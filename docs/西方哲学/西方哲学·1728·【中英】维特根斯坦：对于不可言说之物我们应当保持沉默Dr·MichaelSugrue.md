### 6. Meaning as Use and the Problem of Defining 'Game' (意义即使用与“游戏”定义问题)

Remarkable change of intellectual orientation comes here. Now, the question is, if we're to take this idea of language games seriously, we have to figure out what we would mean by a language game, how these games will work, and what it is that's different in this second conception, this conception of the language game, that makes it different from the conception that he had earlier. Two of Wittgenstein's favorite questions come into play here. One of his favorite questions is, how is a word learned? That is to say, if you want to define, if you want to know about the meaning of a word, X, then ask yourself, how do you find out about X? How would you teach X to a six-year-old? Because as a matter of practical, empirical fact, all the six-year-olds, all the children in our culture are going to have to be taught the words of English, and they get taught them one by one.

这里发生了显著的智识转向。现在的问题是，如果我们要认真对待语言游戏这一观念，我们必须弄清楚我们所说的语言游戏是什么意思，这些游戏将如何运作，以及这第二种观念，即语言游戏观念，与他早先的观念有何不同。维特根斯坦最喜欢的两个问题在这里发挥作用。他最喜欢的问题之一是：一个词是如何被学会的？也就是说，如果你想定义，如果你想了解一个词X的意义，那么问问自己，你如何发现X？你如何向一个六岁的孩子教授X？因为作为实际的、经验的事实，我们文化中的所有六岁孩子，所有儿童，都必须逐个学习英语单词。

Ask yourself how you would teach a word. That will tell you a great deal about what the word means and what the word signifies. In addition to that, he wants to know, a second question that Wittgenstein is particularly interested in is how is a word used? One of the slogans that was derived from this a little later in Wittgenstein's development was, don't ask for the meaning, ask for the use. If you want to know what X means, ask yourself what people do with it. Go look at people using the word X and find out what it's good for. And when you know how they use it, well, you know what it means. This change from the logical necessity of language to its practical contingency is a fascinating turnabout.

问问自己你会如何教一个词。那会告诉你很多关于这个词的意义和它所指的东西。此外，维特根斯坦特别感兴趣的第二个问题是：一个词是如何被使用的？后来在维特根斯坦思想发展中衍生出的一个口号是：不要问意义，要问用法。如果你想知道X是什么意思，问问人们用它做什么。去观察人们使用X这个词，找出它的用途。当你知道了他们如何使用它，你就知道了它的意义。这种从语言的逻辑必然性到其实用偶然性的转变，是一个迷人的转折。

How did we teach Johnny the term game and how do we know when Johnny understands the word game? And what does it mean for us to define the word game? Well, we'd have to drop back a little bit and see what Vicki Stein is fighting against in the Western philosophical tradition. In other words, what's the definition of definition?

我们是如何教约翰尼“游戏”这个词的？我们如何知道约翰尼何时理解了“游戏”这个词？我们定义“游戏”这个词意味着什么？嗯，我们得稍微退后一步，看看维特根斯坦在西方哲学传统中反对的是什么。换句话说，定义的定义是什么？

In the Western philosophical tradition, a kind of meta question, kind of bootstraps us up into a new level. And the best example, the best contrast you can find is in the works of Plato. Socrates is constantly asking people, what is justice? What is goodness? What is virtue? What is beauty? And what he's asking for is a definition, because in each of the Socratic dialogues, there's an attempt to define some important abstract concept.

在西方哲学传统中，有一种元问题，它把我们提升到一个新的层面。最好的例子，你能找到的最佳对比，出现在柏拉图的作品中。苏格拉底不断地问人们：什么是正义？什么是善？什么是德性？什么是美？他所要求的是一个定义，因为在每一场苏格拉底对话中，都试图定义某个重要的抽象概念。

And in fact, in the process of defining it, Socrates has very clear criteria for what counts as a definition. Those of you who are familiar with the Republic know that Socrates wants to define justice. Those of you who are familiar with Ephedrus know that Socrates wants to define love. Those of you who are familiar with the Mino know that Socrates wants to define virtue.

事实上，在定义的过程中，苏格拉底对于什么构成定义有非常明确的标准。你们中熟悉《理想国》的人知道，苏格拉底想要定义正义。你们中熟悉《斐德罗篇》的人知道，苏格拉底想要定义爱。你们中熟悉《美诺篇》的人知道，苏格拉底想要定义德性。

When Socrates in the Mino asks Mino, what is virtue? Mino gives him a bunch of examples. There's the virtue of generals, the virtue of soldiers, the virtue of men, the virtue of women. And Socrates says, no, no, no, stop, stop. I don't want examples of virtue. I want to know what virtue itself is. I want to know some quality that every virtue has and only virtue has. In other words, Socrates demands that virtue be defined in the way that we define odd numbers or triangles. He wants the canons for definition to be strictly mathematical. It goes back to the fact that the Greeks were primarily concerned with mathematical epistemological questions, or at least Plato was.

当苏格拉底在《美诺篇》中问美诺什么是德性时，美诺给了他一大堆例子。有将军的德性、士兵的德性、男人的德性、女人的德性。苏格拉底说：不，不，不，停，停。我不要德性的例子。我想知道德性本身是什么。我想知道某种每个德性都具有、且只有德性才具有的性质。换句话说，苏格拉底要求德性被定义的方式，就像我们定义奇数或三角形那样。他希望定义的标准严格地数学化。这可以追溯到希腊人主要关注数学认识论问题，或者至少柏拉图是这样。

And Socrates wins many arguments by showing that nobody understands what virtue or justice or beauty is because no one can tell him what the form of it is. No one can tell him what every instantiation of beauty has and only instantiations of beauty has. No one can tell him what every example of virtue has and only examples of virtue have. It's not an accident that Socrates, whenever he gives an example of what he wants a definition to be, always chooses some mathematical entity. Mathematical things lend themselves to that kind of definition. The difficulty that Wittgenstein has stumbled on, and it's a fascinating problem, is that many things are not and cannot be defined that way. In other words, he thinks Socrates is unintentionally playing intellectual sleight of hand by demanding of people something that couldn't possibly be there and then it turns out that when the people try and give him what he wants they end up looking stupid because they're on a they're on an expedition to do the impossible a remarkable insight wittgenstein offers us a new idea let's go back to johnny and the game johnny understands the game and what's remarkable is that none of us sat down and said johnny every game has the following quality and only games have the following qualities johnny this is the form of the game

苏格拉底通过表明没有人理解德性、正义或美是什么而赢得许多争论，因为没有人能告诉他这些事物的“形式”是什么。没有人能告诉他，每一个美的实例都具有、且只有美的实例才具有的性质是什么。没有人能告诉他，每一个德性的例子都具有、且只有德性的例子才具有的性质是什么。苏格拉底每当他举出他想要的定义的例子时，总是选择某个数学实体，这不是偶然的。数学事物适合那种定义。维特根斯坦偶然发现了一个迷人的问题，那就是许多事物不能也不应以那种方式被定义。换句话说，他认为苏格拉底无意中玩了智识上的花招，他向人们要求某种根本不可能存在的东西，然后当人们试图给他想要的东西时，他们最终看起来很愚蠢，因为他们是在进行一场不可能完成的远征。一个非凡的洞见，维特根斯坦为我们提供了一个新的观念。让我们回到约翰尼和游戏。约翰尼理解游戏，而值得注意的是，我们没有人坐下来对他说：约翰尼，每个游戏都具有以下性质，且只有游戏才具有以下性质；约翰尼，这就是游戏的形式。

As a matter of fact, if called upon to tell someone what the form of the game is, in other words, if called upon to say some quality that every game has and only games have, I think you'd run into all kinds of problems. Ask yourself what the Olympics have in common with tic-tac-toe. Ask yourself what poker has in common with chess. Ask yourself what tag has in common with peekaboo. Ask yourself if any of these things have one universal characteristic that allows us to say that all these things are games. Wittgenstein has opened up a can of skeptical worms here, and he's going to close it, too. Quite remarkable.

事实上，如果被要求告诉别人游戏的形式是什么，换句话说，如果被要求说出某种每个游戏都具有、且只有游戏才具有的性质，我想你会遇到各种问题。问问自己，奥运会和井字棋有什么共同点。问问自己，扑克牌和国际象棋有什么共同点。问问自己，捉人游戏和躲猫猫有什么共同点。问问自己，这些东西是否有一个普遍特征，使我们能够说所有这些都是游戏。如果它们没有一个普遍特征，我们怎么能以有意义的方式使用这个词呢？把所有这些都描述为游戏意味着什么？维特根斯坦在这里打开了一罐怀疑论的蠕虫，他也会把它关上。相当了不起。

让我们回到约翰尼和我们的游戏。我们教约翰尼躲猫猫。我们教约翰尼也许一个纸牌游戏。我们教约翰尼下跳棋。我们教约翰尼许多游戏。我们怎么知道约翰尼何时理解了“游戏”这个词？我们所做的是寻找某种行为，某种约翰尼的使用，表明他知道这个词在我们的语言中的位置。例如，如果他开始写作业，他说：我正在写作业用的这个东西，那是一个游戏。我们说：不，约翰尼，你不明白。那不是游戏，那是一支铅笔。游戏是像捉人、跳棋或纸牌之类的东西，约翰尼。但注意，我们不说：约翰尼，以下是游戏的形式。我们给约翰尼例子。

### 7. Ostensive Teaching and the Fuzziness of Language (实指教学与语言的模糊性)

So what Wittgenstein is saying here is that as a matter of practical fact, all of us, not just Johnny, but all of us, learn the use of words like game, everyday simple things like that, by being given examples. This is called ostensive definition. In other words, in contrast to the Socratic essential definition, by specifying one quality that every X has and only X has, Wittgenstein wants to offer us a new paradigm that will cover some kinds of definition. In fact, it will cover most kinds of definition. This will be definition by giving examples of something. So if you want to teach someone that doesn't understand English, speaks a foreign language, what a chair is, you point out examples of chairs. You say, sir, that's a chair, and sir, that's a chair, and that's a chair, and that's a chair, and that's a chair.

所以维特根斯坦在这里所说的是，作为一个实际事实，我们所有人，不仅仅是约翰尼，而是我们所有人，都是通过被给予例子来学习像“游戏”这样的词的用法，以及诸如此类的日常简单事物。这被称为实指定义。换句话说，与苏格拉底式的本质定义相对照，即通过指定某种每个X都具有、且只有X才具有的性质来定义，维特根斯坦想要提供一个新的范式，以涵盖某些类型的定义。事实上，它将涵盖大多数类型的定义。这就是通过给出某物的例子来定义。所以，如果你想教一个不懂英语、说外语的人什么是椅子，你会指出椅子的例子。你说，先生，那是椅子，先生，那是椅子，那是椅子，那是椅子，那是椅子。

And then at some point in time, you get tired of saying, that's a chair, that's a chair, that's a chair, then you stop. And then after that, if you want to find out if this person understands the word chair, you ask them, could you use that in a sentence and point out a few things? And if they point to the sky or if they point to a mountain, you understand that they don't understand what you meant by chair and you have to give them some more examples. If, on the other hand, he says, that's a chair, that's a chair, that's a chair, then you have as good a reason as you are ever going to have to think that he understands the word chair.

然后在某个时刻，你厌倦了说“那是椅子，那是椅子，那是椅子”，然后你停下来。之后，如果你想弄清楚这个人是否理解了“椅子”这个词，你问他们：你能用那个词造个句子并指出一些东西吗？如果他们指向天空或指向一座山，你就明白他们没有理解你说的“椅子”是什么意思，你必须再给他们一些例子。另一方面，如果他说“那是椅子，那是椅子，那是椅子”，那么你就有充分的理由认为他理解了“椅子”这个词，这是你所能拥有的最好的理由。

Confuse us and baffle us with the most profound intellectual constructs, Wittgenstein can baffle us with the contents of our closets. Wittgenstein can baffle us with the objects on the table. Wittgenstein can make it amazing that you should know how to use the word sugar or salt. Remarkable, but not remarkable. What Wittgenstein has done is gone back to the way words actually work in the practical empirical world. He started out his career as an engineer. And then he moved into mathematics. And the desire for mathematical formality sacrificed common sense. And now this is a revival of common sense. This is common sense in a way with a vengeance. Think about the paradigm of six-year-olds. If they understand what game means, don't tell me that us philosophers don't. Now there's a problem here, and Plato would be the first one to come back, let's be fair to him. He'd say, look, how do we know when we really understand something? Maybe Johnny is going to go out and misuse the word later on, and there's an intrinsic ambiguity to defining things by example. That is to say, there's always going to be an uncertainty when you get to the peripheral examples of a game or a chair or anything else as to whether this falls under the rubric chair or game or not. ### 8. Family Resemblance and Responding to Plato and Frege (家族相似性：回应柏拉图与弗雷格)

### 9. Understanding as Behavior and Philosophy as Therapy (作为行为的理解与作为疗法的哲学)

Now, there's a number of difficulties here. Difficulty number one, actually there are a plethora of difficulties, that's not fair, but the difficulty that immediately suggests itself. How do we know when someone understands a term? Well, we know when they can use the term the way we use the term. And if they start using the term in a funny way, that is to say, if Johnny starts pointing at his pencil and saying, this is a game, we say, no, Johnny, for the hundredth time, that's a pencil, that's not a game. Well, here's the idea.

In other words, this theory of whether you understand the term game or not refers not at all to your mental experiences, to your subjective understanding of it. It refers to the way you behave. So in other words, it comes perilously close to turning into a kind of philosophical behaviorism.

一个显著的突破。其困难在于它倾向于将我们锁在自己的心理之中，并且倾向于使行为难以提供令人满意的理由来相信某些类型的经验。维特根斯坦在《哲学研究》中说：你的哲学的目标是什么？让苍蝇飞出捕蝇瓶。

In other words, we're taken in by the language that we've been brought up on, and we tend to get confused because language itself is full of odd quirks and odd facts which only can be determined and only can be unraveled in the way words get used. So back to the theme. Don't ask for the meaning. Ask for the use. If you're still unclear as to what you're doing, then ask, how is this word learned?

换句话说，我们被我们成长过程中使用的语言所迷惑，我们倾向于变得困惑，因为语言本身充满了奇怪的怪癖和奇怪的事实，这些只能通过词语被使用的方式来确定和揭示。所以回到主题。不要问意义。问用途。如果你仍然不清楚自己在做什么，那么问：这个词是如何学会的？

So for Wittgenstein, philosophy in the investigations turns out to be a kind of therapy. We have conceptual charley horses, or we have conceptual diseases, and he's going to be a trainer or intellectual doctor who's going to help us improve our thinking, and he's going to offer us therapy for the misapprehensions and the confusions introduced into our thinking by our use of language. ### 10. Pragmatic Standards and the Legacy of Wittgenstein (实用标准与维特根斯坦的遗产)

The problem with the investigations is that it's incomplete. One of the difficulties is, and here's the real point, is that there's an infinite number of overlapping language games, and not only is it incomplete, but philosophy itself is incomplete. The incompleteness of this book gestures at the fact that there is no ultimate finale to the philosophical enterprise, which is a total reversal of what he thought he had done in the early part of his career. He thought he had solved all philosophical problems. There's nothing left to talk about. Everything else is just mopping up the confusions that have to be left over from 2,500 years of Platonism. Now we know not only is philosophy not ending, but it's never ending.

《哲学研究》的问题在于它是不完整的。其中一个困难，也是真正的要点，在于存在着无限多的重叠的语言游戏，而且不仅这本书不完整，哲学本身也是不完整的。这本书的不完整性暗示着哲学事业没有终极的终局，这与他早年认为自己已经完成的事业完全相反。他曾以为自己解决了所有哲学问题，没有什么可谈的了，剩下的只是清理两千五百年柏拉图主义遗留下来的混乱。现在我们知道了，哲学不仅没有终结，而且永远不会终结。

It's never ending because human puzzlement never ends. And in fact, there is no one universal skeleton key. There's no one universal thread which leads us out of the labyrinth of language. All we can do is learn the local rules used in various communities and in various forms of life. with regard to certain linguistic activities. And once Johnny learns how to use the word chair and refers to things like that with it, well then, Johnny understands what a chair is. And that is true not just for chairs, but it's true for all of language. And what he's done is undeceive us.

它永远不会终结，因为人类的困惑永无止境。事实上，并不存在一把万能的钥匙，也没有一条普适的线索引我们走出语言的迷宫。我们所能做的只是学习各个共同体和各种生活形式中与特定语言活动相关的局部规则。一旦小约翰学会了使用“椅子”这个词，并用它来指代像那样的东西，那么他就理解了什么是椅子。这不仅适用于椅子，也适用于所有语言。而他所做的，就是让我们不再受骗。

And make unambiguous things which we thought were problems but in fact are not problems. The problem lies not with the world, the problem lies not with language, the problem lies with the unreasonable set of demands and expectations that we bring to it. The therapy that he is performing in the large scale sense is to rid us of the platonic demand for ultimate certainty and go to an essentially pragmatic standard which says if it works, it works. If it isn't broke, don't fix it. If Johnny can use the word chair, then Johnny understands chair, whether he can tell you what the form of the chair is or not. As a matter of fact, it may be a waste of time because in all probability there is no form of the chair.

并且把那些我们以为是问题、其实并非问题的事情变得清晰明了。问题不在于世界，不在于语言，而在于我们带给它的那些不合理的要求和期望。他在宏观意义上所做的治疗，是让我们摆脱对终极确定性的柏拉图式要求，转向一种本质上的实用标准，即：如果它管用，那就管用。如果没坏，就别修。如果小约翰能用“椅子”这个词，那他就理解了椅子，无论他能否告诉你椅子的形式是什么。事实上，那可能是浪费时间，因为很可能根本不存在椅子的形式。

There is no one quality which every chair has and only chairs have. All we have is the overlapping language games. Some of them are more precise than others. If some are found too ambiguous for our purposes, we can increase the precision with which we use language at any time, and we can increase it to the point where we find it useful again. And then there's no point in taking our precision any further except for points of pedantry, because if it isn't fixed, if it isn't broke, don't fix it.

不存在一种所有椅子都具有、且只有椅子才具有的性质。我们拥有的只是重叠的语言游戏。有些游戏比其他游戏更精确。如果发现某些游戏对我们的目的来说过于模糊，我们可以随时提高语言使用的精确度，直到它再次变得有用为止。再进一步追求精确就没有意义了，除非是为了卖弄学问，因为如果它没坏，就别修。

He rescues us from centuries of unnecessary worry. He helps us avoid a lifetime of the frustrating pursuit of something that's not there. He says, or in a way, it's a way of going back to the first line of the Tractatus, the world is all that is the case. And what we cannot speak about, we must pass over in silence.

他把我们从几个世纪以来不必要的忧虑中解救出来。他帮助我们避免一生徒劳地追求某种并不存在的东西。他说，或者说在某种意义上，这是回到《逻辑哲学论》的第一句话：世界就是所发生的一切。而对于我们不能言说的，我们必须保持沉默。

In the Tractatus, being a logical, scientific kind of a guy, he said that there were no way that we could express propositions in ethics or in theology or in aesthetics. It's too gooey and ambiguous for him. Later on, he comes back with this idea of the fuzzy photo. We can talk about ethics. We can talk about aesthetics. We can talk about theology. The difficulty is that they're very ambiguous, very fuzzy words. The question is, is the fuzziness of our discourse an impediment to understanding each other or not? If we get an understanding of what someone is talking about when they talk about beauty or about God or about religion, if it communicates with you and you can tell by whether they use it in the proper way, well, then it's working just fine. If it's too ambiguous for our purposes, then sharpen it up.

在《逻辑哲学论》中，作为一个逻辑的、科学的人，他说我们无法在伦理学、神学或美学中表达命题。那对他来说太黏糊、太模糊了。后来，他带着这个模糊照片的想法回来了。我们可以谈论伦理学，可以谈论美学，可以谈论神学。困难在于它们是非常模糊、非常含混的词。问题是，我们话语的模糊性是否妨碍了彼此理解？如果我们能理解某人在谈论美、上帝或宗教时所说的内容，如果它能与你交流，并且你能通过他们是否以恰当的方式使用它来判断，那么它就运作得很好。如果对我们的目的来说过于模糊，那就把它磨锋利。

we are rescued not only from an extreme skepticism, we are rescued from chasing our own tails around like puppies. In that respect, Wittgenstein represents a retreat from philosophical hubris. He is a hero in the Socratic sense because he's not only willing to criticize others, he's willing to criticize himself, and he's unwilling to accept the flattery that comes with philosophical breakthroughs when you yourself have conscientious misgivings about what you've written. As an example of intellectual honesty, Ludwig Wittgenstein is without peer in the last 500 years of philosophy. One of the greatest thinkers, and in that respect, one of the greatest souls of all. And as a matter of just empirical personal fact for him, he was one of the loneliest and most, how can I put it, distant of men.

我们不仅被从极端的怀疑论中解救出来，还被从像小狗一样追逐自己尾巴的徒劳中解救出来。在这方面，维特根斯坦代表了对哲学傲慢的退却。他是苏格拉底意义上的英雄，因为他不仅愿意批评他人，也愿意批评自己，而且当自己对自己的著作有良心上的疑虑时，他不愿接受伴随哲学突破而来的奉承。作为智识诚实的典范，路德维希·维特根斯坦在过去五百年的哲学中无与伦比。他是最伟大的思想家之一，就此而言，也是最伟大的灵魂之一。而就他个人的经验事实而言，他是最孤独、最疏离的人之一。

And part of it comes from this extreme logical rigor, part of it comes from this willingness to ask new questions, willingness to even undermine one's own conclusions, but it also connects to the fact that Wittgenstein himself wanted to bring philosophy down to this world and it amounts to the idea that what we cannot speak about we must pass over in silence and the domain of silence, it turns out in the investigations, is not as great as we had thought.

部分原因来自这种极端的逻辑严谨性，部分来自这种愿意提出新问题、甚至愿意破坏自己结论的态度，但这也与维特根斯坦本人想把哲学拉回尘世有关，这归结为这样一个观念：凡我们不能言说的，就必须保持沉默，而沉默的领域，在《哲学研究》中看来，并不像我们曾经以为的那么大。
