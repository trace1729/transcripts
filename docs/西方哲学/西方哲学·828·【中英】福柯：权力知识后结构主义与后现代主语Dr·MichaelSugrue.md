- In The Order of Things, Foucault introduces the concept of 'episteme'—a historically contingent paradigm of knowledge—with arbitrary, discontinuous breaks between epochs.
- Foucault's later works, Discipline and Punish and The History of Sexuality, extend his critique to penology and sexuality, viewing them as socially constructed mechanisms of power.
- Critics, including Habermas, point out that Foucault's radical historicism and social constructivism lead to nihilism, making liberation and coercion equally acceptable.
- Despite these criticisms, Foucault's work has been highly influential in reconceptualizing psychiatry, penology, and sexuality studies.

他吸收了大量的20世纪之前的影响，然后取20世纪法国文化的全部范围，加以挑选，试图将其整合在一起。令人惊叹的是，他在探究过程中涵盖了如此多的领域。他当然引入了索雷尔。索雷尔如今已不太被阅读，但他在20世纪初为暴力和恐怖主义进行了辩护。福柯并非对暴力和恐怖主义本身感兴趣。我认为他不会赞同，也不会反对。他认为这并不那么有趣。相反，他从索雷尔那里借用的是唯意志论的思想，即为了某个政治目标而任意意愿，无需任何合法化原则，也不存在这种原则的可能性。这是索雷尔和福柯的关键问题之一。

And of course, Heidegger, as is the case for all contemporary thinkers, Heidegger is of great importance. And his borrowing from Heidegger is most remarkable. Heidegger was an anti-humanist, and he explicitly tried to construct a philosophy which would get beyond the modern ideals of the human and the humane towards some new conception of the subject, some new conception of interiority. And Foucault borrowed that. In other words, Foucault is explicitly and repeatedly anti-humanistic in his outlook. Foucault once said that humanism is everything that restricts the desire for power. Well, you must then decide whether you wish to pursue power exclusively and develop a sort of Nietzschean anti-humanism, or whether you mean to go back to the moral and political organizing principles of the Enlightenment, like we get with Habermas, in which case we're willing to accept some rational restriction on our desire for power.

当然，海德格尔，正如对所有当代思想家一样，对福柯也极为重要。他对海德格尔的借用最为显著。海德格尔是反人道主义者，他明确试图构建一种哲学，超越现代的人道与人性理想，走向某种新的主体概念，某种新的内在性概念。福柯借用了这一点。换句话说，福柯明确且反复地持反人道主义观点。福柯曾说，人道主义是限制权力欲望的一切。那么，你必须决定是希望纯粹追求权力并发展一种尼采式的反人道主义，还是打算回到启蒙运动的道德和政治组织原则，就像哈贝马斯那样，在这种情况下，我们愿意接受对权力欲望的某种理性限制。

The final important idea for Foucault, a source of his ideas, is structuralism. And what's important about Foucault's borrowings from structuralism is that structuralism tends towards the abolition of human agency. In other words, in structuralist histories, and there aren't very many of them, thank God, but in attempts to create structuralist theories of history, like we get with, say, Althusser, the result is that it turns the historical process into a vast blind, atelological machine where agency is deprived from individual human individuals, but agency is also removed from nations, from classes, from political or moral or social tendencies. In other words, there is no agency involved. There's no self, there's no freedom, there's no point or site of moral responsibility. It just is what it is. So Foucault has borrowed that idea from structuralism, the idea of abolishing agency, and that hooks up very nicely with his idea of abolishing not only modernity, but abolishing the modern conception of man, the modern idea of humanism as well.

对福柯而言，最后一个重要的思想来源是结构主义。福柯从结构主义中借用之处的重要性在于，结构主义倾向于废除人的能动性。换句话说，在结构主义的历史中——这样的历史并不多，谢天谢地——但在试图创建结构主义历史理论时，比如阿尔都塞的理论，结果是历史过程变成了一个巨大的、盲目的、无目的论的机器，能动性被剥夺，不仅从个体的人身上剥夺，也从民族、阶级、政治或道德或社会趋势中剥夺。换句话说，没有能动性。没有自我，没有自由，没有道德责任的位置或场所。它就是这样。因此，福柯从结构主义借用了废除能动性的观念，这与他的废除现代性、废除现代人的概念、废除现代人道主义观念的想法完美契合。

### 5. Madness and Civilization: The Social Construction of Madness (疯癫与文明：疯癫的社会建构)

Now, it's worth having a detailed look at some of his major works because many of these themes, which are in abstract so hard to comprehend, make a little more sense when you put them in context. Now, there's something very ironic and playful and perverse about Foucault and you have to kind of like that in his work. His first major book, Madness and Civilization, was a study of madness in the Age of Reason. And there's something bitingly ironic about the idea, because of course the Age of Reason is the age of Kant, the age of Descartes, the age of the great rationalist thinkers, Spinoza, and what does...

现在，值得详细审视他的一些主要著作，因为许多抽象上难以理解的主题，在具体语境中会变得更有意义。福柯的作品中有某种讽刺、嬉戏和反常的东西，你必须有点喜欢这一点。他的第一本主要著作《疯癫与文明》是对理性时代疯癫的研究。这个想法带有尖锐的讽刺意味，因为理性时代当然是康德的时代，笛卡尔的时代，伟大的理性主义思想家斯宾诺莎的时代，而什么……

Foucault want to look at? Not the advent of rationality, not the increase of reason or the development of natural science or anything that we could plausibly attribute progressive development to. Instead, what he wants to look at is madness. And particularly, this is important, he wants to look at the social and linguistic construction of madness. Here's what's important there. Foucault was, in trying to extend the domain of human freedom, wanted to remove the legitimacy of as many possible restrictions on human freedom as possible. Some of these restrictions on human freedom will be outmoded moral codes will be old-fashioned political coercion, but also there will be a tendency to, in Foucault's work, to denounce nature itself, to try and fly from nature because the limitations imposed upon human freedom by physical natural reality are just one more thing that Foucault objects to.

福柯想考察的是什么？不是理性的到来，不是理性的增长或自然科学的发展，也不是任何我们可以合理地归因于进步性发展的事物。相反，他想考察的是疯狂。尤其重要的是，他想考察疯狂的社会建构和语言建构。这里的关键在于：福柯在试图拓展人类自由的领域时，希望尽可能多地消除对人类自由的限制的合法性。其中一些对人类自由的限制将是过时的道德规范，将是过时的政治强制，但在福柯的作品中，还有一种倾向，即谴责自然本身，试图逃离自然，因为物理自然现实对人类自由施加的限制，只是福柯所反对的又一件事情。

In other words, he wishes to extend the whole domain of human freedom. The way to do that is to remove and delegitimize any discourse which claims to situate itself in nature. It's a powerful and very important intellectual gambit. In other words, if someone claims that the inequality of wealth in advanced capitalist society is natural, what you do is you inquire into the historical development of advanced capitalism and the differential distribution of wealth, and you show that no, in fact, this did not come out of nature, whatever that is, but rather it is socially constructed, it is constructed in the process of contingent historical reality.

换言之，他希望拓展人类自由的整个领域。这样做的方法是移除并剥夺任何声称自身植根于自然的话语的合法性。这是一个强大且非常重要的智识策略。换句话说，如果有人声称发达资本主义社会中财富的不平等是自然的，你要做的就是探究发达资本主义的历史发展以及财富的差异分配，并表明：不，事实上，这并非源于自然（无论自然是什么），而是社会建构的，是在偶然的历史现实的进程中建构起来的。

Now let's think about this and go back to the idea of madness. What Foucault says is that in the age of reason, this age of rational humanitarianism, this age of enlightened freedom and the extension of the domain of human capacity, what was really going on during the Enlightenment, what was really going on when we created the origins of this idea of madness, what we were doing is coercing the previously free. So in other words, although the history of ideas frequently treats the history of insane asylums and the history of the psychiatric diagnosis and treatment of madness as being a progressive thing, as being a humane thing, as being a morally desirable thing, Foucault says no. What you did when we constructed the idea of madness, socially constructed it, linguistically constructed it, what we did was we imposed a certain kind of coercion. We imposed a certain element of the will to power of a certain element of society on another element of society. And we invented this bogus discourse called madness and the discourse of madness and the discourse which legitimizes putting people in madhouses.

现在让我们思考这一点，并回到疯狂的概念。福柯说，在理性的时代，这个理性人道主义的时代，这个启蒙自由和人类能力领域拓展的时代，在启蒙运动期间真正发生的事情，在我们创造疯狂这一观念的起源时真正发生的事情，是我们正在强制那些先前自由的人。因此，尽管思想史常常将疯人院的历史以及精神病学对疯狂的诊断和治疗的历史视为进步的事物，视为人道的事物，视为道德上可取的事物，但福柯说不。当我们建构疯狂的观念时——社会地建构它，语言地建构它——我们所做的是施加某种强制。我们将社会中某一群体对另一群体的权力意志的某种要素强加于人。我们发明了这种虚假的话语，称为疯狂，以及疯狂的话语，以及使将人关进疯人院合法化的话语。

Foucault, historically looking at it, says prior to the advent of the Enlightenment, prior to the age of, or prior to say, I don't know, say 1650 to 1800 when the Enlightenment really is in full bloom, prior to that we had what he called a toleration for madness, even a like of madness. He goes back and look at Renaissance texts and he's right about this. If you go back and look at Erasmus, or you go back and look at, say, Moore's Utopia, you will find that the mad or the insane are treated as sport, the subject of jest perhaps, but they are not treated in an undignified way. Madness is a common human peril perhaps, but there is not a special discourse which legitimizes locking these people up.

福柯从历史的角度看，在启蒙运动到来之前，在理性时代之前，或者说在1650年到1800年启蒙运动真正全面展开之前，我们拥有他所谓的对疯狂的容忍，甚至是对疯狂的喜爱。他回顾文艺复兴时期的文本，他在这方面是对的。如果你回顾伊拉斯谟，或者回顾莫尔的《乌托邦》，你会发现疯人或精神错乱者被视为玩笑的对象，或许成为戏谑的主题，但他们并未受到有失尊严的对待。疯狂或许是人类共同的危险，但并没有一种特殊的话语使关押这些人合法化。

On the basis of social utility and taxing all of us on that ground. In other words, we don't have, we have not in the time of the Renaissance constructed an interest in stigmatizing and marginalizing the mad. That is a product of the Enlightenment. And what Foucault is driving at here is that the Enlightenment is hidden coercion. What is described as the advent of reason, the increase in human freedom, the progressive development of humanity and justice and kindness is in fact a stalking horse for the will to power. So what Foucault is saying is that we have artificially and socially constructed this idea of madness and we use it to coerce certain kinds of people for certain sorts of reasons.

基于社会效用并以之向我们所有人征税。换句话说，在文艺复兴时期，我们并没有建构一种污名化和边缘化疯人的利益。那是启蒙运动的产物。福柯在这里要指出的是，启蒙运动是隐藏的强制。被描述为理性的到来、人类自由的增长、人类、正义和仁慈的进步发展的东西，实际上是权力意志的掩护。所以福柯所说的是，我们人为地、社会地建构了疯狂这一观念，并利用它来出于某种原因强制某些类型的人。

Now clearly, I think Foucault is looking back to his own early experiences. Can you mention Foucault and his analyst? And Foucault is doubtless a great deal more intelligent than his analyst, but their inability to communicate and Foucault's experience of someone trying to coerce him... doubtless helped him formulate his stance towards being his principled refusal to be governed. So it is a courageous and thoughtful philosophy for all its perversity and for all its morbid elements.

现在，显然，我认为福柯是在回顾他自己的早期经历。你能提到福柯和他的精神分析师吗？福柯无疑比他的分析师聪明得多，但他们无法沟通，以及福柯经历有人试图强制他……无疑帮助他形成了他的立场，即原则上拒绝被治理。因此，尽管有其反常和病态之处，这仍是一种勇敢而深思熟虑的哲学。

reconceptualizations of psychiatry. The source, the inspiration for their work is that of Foucault, at least his early texts. So he has been very fruitful and very influential within academic circles. In other words, he proposes questions which intellectuals, for some reason, seem to gravitate towards, find very fertile and fruitful and useful. And the results of this has been that he's cross-fertilized various fields where you wouldn't have expected him to show up. In addition, in practice, the movement towards the deinstitutionalization of the people who were stigmatized as mad that's gone over, that's happened for the last 10 or 15 or 20 years, owes a great deal of its impulse, of its impetus, to Foucault's writing and to the writings that he'd spawned and generated along these lines.

精神病学的重新概念化。他们工作的来源和灵感是福柯，至少是他的早期文本。因此，他在学术界非常富有成果且极具影响力。换句话说，他提出的问题，知识分子们似乎出于某种原因倾向于这些问题，发现它们非常肥沃、富有成果且有用。结果是，他在你意想不到的领域进行了交叉融合。此外，在实践中，去机构化运动——即那些被污名化为疯人的人的去机构化——在过去10年、15年或20年里已经发生，其动力和推动力在很大程度上归功于福柯的著作以及他沿着这些思路催生和产生的著作。

### 6. The Order of Things and the Concept of Episteme (词与物与认识型概念)

Now, after spending some time discussing madness, discussing the clinic, discussing the course of elements in psychiatry, Foucault then in 1966 produces his greatest work. It's called The Order of Things. In French, it's called Words and Things. And what Foucault is doing in this text, it's a very deep and difficult text if you are in encountering Foucault for the first time, I do not recommend that you go first to the order of things. Read some of the stuff like the Birth of the Clinic, which is a little more accessible, makes a little more sense for the beginner.

在花了一些时间讨论疯狂、讨论临床医学、讨论精神病学要素的课程之后，福柯于1966年创作了他最伟大的著作。它被称为《词与物》。在法语中，它被称为《词与物》。福柯在这本书中所做的——如果你第一次接触福柯，这是一本非常深奥难懂的文本，我不建议你先读《词与物》。读一些像《临床医学的诞生》这样的东西，它更容易理解一些，对初学者来说更有意义。

But in the order of things Foucault treats the history of Western thought from, oh, about, I guess, 1500 or so to the present, breaks it into four general periodizations, and talks about specifically the soft sciences, the human sciences, and the way they changed over time. What he does is he looks for patterns in the changes that we find in economics, the patterns of changes that we find in biology, and the patterns of changes that we find in linguistics.

但在事物的秩序中，福柯将西方思想史——大约从1500年左右至今——划分为四个大致时期，并特别讨论了软科学、人文科学及其随时间的变化。他所做的是寻找我们在经济学、生物学和语言学中发现的变化模式。

So what Foucault does is analyze the writings that are being produced, the thinking that's being produced in these various fields, and then try and discuss how we socially construct and reconstruct these fields over time and to see if there is any simultaneity or connectedness between the way in which these transformations occur. What he finds is something like this. Last 500 years or so, the West has been under the aegis of four basic epistems. Now, what he means by episteme is, in some respects, analogous to what Hegel meant when he talked about phases of the geist. Right, different phases, different stances towards looking at the world. Another way of thinking about Foucault's idea of episteme would be something like a grammar or a way of, a grammar of interpretation or a conceptual grid analogous to Thomas Kuhn's idea of paradigms. So one paradigm of knowledge reigns supreme at any given time. There may be variances within knowledge. It may not be completely monolithic, but it will cover the greater part of the domain.

因此，福柯所做的是分析这些不同领域中产生的著作和思想，然后试图讨论我们如何在社会上建构和重构这些领域，并观察这些转变方式之间是否存在同时性或关联性。他发现的情况大致如下：在过去500年左右，西方一直处于四种基本认识型的支配之下。他所说的认识型，在某些方面类似于黑格尔谈论精神阶段时的含义。不同的阶段，不同的看待世界的立场。另一种理解福柯认识型概念的方式是将其视为一种语法或解释的语法，或一种概念网格，类似于托马斯·库恩的范式概念。因此，在任何给定时间，一种知识范式占据主导地位。知识内部可能存在差异，它可能不是完全铁板一块，但它将覆盖该领域的大部分。

And what Foucault says is that there are four of these epistems and, like Kuhn, he says that the breaks between epistems are not generated by any logical transformative structure. In other words, here's another one of his breaks from structuralism. Structuralism demands that we have rules for the transformation of one structure into another. What Foucault says is that no, history isn't well enough organized to do that. Instead, these changes, the alteration from one episteme to another is just plain discontinuous. There are no rules for the structural transformation of one episteme into another. And what this means is that we almost get a parody of Hegel's Phenomenology of Geist, where Hegel tries to convince us all that the change from one outlook to another is necessary, and that thesis generates its antithesis, and then they get synthesized, and then we continue the process again. What we get with Foucault is very much the opposite.

福柯认为有四种这样的认识型，并且像库恩一样，他说认识型之间的断裂并非由任何逻辑转换结构产生。换句话说，这是他与结构主义的又一次决裂。结构主义要求我们具备将一种结构转换为另一种结构的规则。福柯说，不，历史并没有组织得那么好。相反，这些变化，从一种认识型到另一种认识型的转变，只是纯粹的不连续。没有规则可以将一种认识型结构性地转换为另一种。这意味着我们几乎得到了黑格尔《精神现象学》的戏仿，黑格尔试图让我们相信从一种观点到另一种观点的变化是必然的，正题产生反题，然后它们被综合，然后我们继续这个过程。而我们从福柯那里得到的恰恰相反。

not only is the transformation from one episteme to another arbitrary and unpredictable, but there is no necessary logical correlation between the two. So, in other words, not only does the change over historical time of human thought not form a progressive whole, it is in principle unpredictable. It is in principle arbitrarily or randomly transformed. If you can imagine looking into a kaleidoscope and then twisting the kaleidoscope slightly and then arbitrarily changing the organization of the pattern you see, that is something like Foucault's idea of historical change. He emphasizes discontinuity. He emphasizes disjunction. He calls these things epistemological breaks and he says that epistemological breaks are simple facts of life and there is no way which we can formulate or formalize the way in which they work.

不仅从一种认识型到另一种认识型的转变是任意且不可预测的，而且两者之间没有必然的逻辑关联。换句话说，人类思想在历史时间中的变化不仅不构成一个进步的整体，而且原则上不可预测。它原则上被任意或随机地改变。如果你能想象看着万花筒，然后轻轻转动万花筒，任意改变你所看到的图案的组织，那就像福柯的历史变化观念。他强调不连续性，强调断裂。他称这些为认识论断裂，并说认识论断裂是生活中的简单事实，我们无法制定或形式化它们运作的方式。

Now the four epistems that Foucault inquires into break down this way. There's the Renaissance episteme, which goes until about 1650, and what he says is the key thing in that episteme or that paradigm is the idea of analogy. All of Renaissance thinking works off the idea of analogy, works off making connections between qualities in two different things, finding that sameness, connecting the two. It's a simple, relatively primitive move, and for its purposes, at the time, it is a satisfactory intellectual organizing principle, but once we get to the Enlightenment, we get a change in that organizing principle, but not for any good reason. In other words, what I guess someone like Hegel would want us to believe is that the reason we move from the age of the Renaissance in...

福柯所探究的四种认识型分解如下：文艺复兴认识型，持续到大约1650年，他说这种认识型或范式的关键思想是类比。所有文艺复兴思想都基于类比观念，基于在两个不同事物之间建立性质上的联系，发现相同性，将两者连接起来。这是一个简单、相对原始的动作，就其当时的目的而言，它是一个令人满意的智力组织原则，但一旦我们进入启蒙运动，这种组织原则就发生了变化，但并非出于任何充分的理由。换句话说，我想像黑格尔这样的人会让我们相信，我们从文艺复兴时代（在福柯看来以类比观念为中心）转向启蒙时代（在福柯看来以分析观念为中心）是有某种必然的逻辑原因的。

Foucault's view, which is centered around the idea of analogy, to the age of the Enlightenment, which in Foucault's view is centered around the idea of analysis. Heygood wants to believe there's some necessary logical reason why we move from one to the other. What Foucault is telling us is no, there may be contingent historical reasons, but none of those make this necessary or predictable. None of this make this any kind of intellectual advance. It's just what comes next. So what we get then is a sort of series of progressive changes in the mental outlook of the West, but they don't have internal telos, they don't have anything that holds them together.

而福柯告诉我们的是，不，可能有偶然的历史原因，但这些都不会使这种转变成为必然或可预测的。这些都不构成任何智力进步。它只是接下来发生的事情。因此，我们得到的是一系列西方精神面貌的渐进变化，但它们没有内在目的论，没有任何东西将它们联系在一起。

### 7. Modernity, Postmodernity, and the End of Man (现代性、后现代性与人的终结)
