It takes discourses that have been ignored and vilified and condemned and marginalized and says they're just as good as any other kind of discourse.

在这种科学中，任何智力活动，无论是自然科学、艺术、政治还是其他，都不会对评判其他替代话语做出总体化的主张。这样做使我们有充分理由抵制那种压制他者的恐怖主义现代倾向。如果你在科学中的目标是产生最大多样性的话语、最大替代视角，那么我们就有了一种新的、自由的、解放的科学，它可以通过让我们对存在其他合法看待世界的方式持开放态度，来帮助迎来下一个时代、下一代人。并且让我们在思考其他替代话语时，不戴这些自我强加的眼罩。换句话说，这将是对边缘性的推崇，这在当今是一个非常重要的词。它把那些被忽视、被诋毁、被谴责、被边缘化的话语，说成和其他任何话语一样好。

And what we want then is a very democratic sort of a theory in the sense that it wants to say that opinion is the same thing as knowledge because we have no way of gesturing at any substantial reality which would legitimize our knowledge. So then there's just persuasion rather than being convinced because you can only be convinced by certain knowledge, substantial reality. There's no such thing. So we just have persuasion. And some people are persuaded of some things. Some people are persuaded of others. Some people are persuaded to look at the world in one way. Some people have different life experiences, different inclinations, are persuaded to look at the world in another way. But there's no ultimate Urstaff that we can gesture at, which would give us good grounds for choosing one over another. You can see how he departs radically from Habermas here, who thinks that the whole point of philosophy is to find some such thing. Elsewise, it ends up being organized chaos.

那么我们想要的就是一种非常民主的理论，因为它想说意见等同于知识，因为我们无法指向任何实质性的现实来使我们的知识合法化。所以只有说服，而不是被说服，因为你只能被确定的知识、实质性的现实所说服。没有这种东西。所以我们只有说服。有些人被说服相信某些事情，有些人被说服相信另一些事情。有些人被说服以某种方式看待世界，有些人由于不同的生活经历、不同的倾向，被说服以另一种方式看待世界。但没有最终的“原质”（Urstaff）可以让我们指向，从而为选择其一而非其他提供充分理由。你可以看到他与哈贝马斯的分歧有多大，哈贝马斯认为哲学的全部意义就在于找到这样的东西。否则，最终会变成有组织的混乱。

### 8. Parology, Alienation, and the Loss of Communication (谬误推理、异化与交流的丧失)

Now, what he's trying to do then, and it's an unfinished question in his book on postmodernism, he wants to ask the question, can parology legitimate a politics of resistance? In other words, can we find a new way of legitimizing our political choices, our moral choices, and our scientific understanding of the world based upon this goal or idea of parology, on this infinite profusion of discourses and perspectives?

那么，他当时试图做的——这也是他关于后现代主义的那本书中一个悬而未决的问题——他想问：谬误推理能否为抵抗的政治提供合法性？换句话说，我们能否找到一种新的方式来为我们的政治选择、道德选择以及我们对世界的科学理解进行辩护，这种辩护基于谬误推理这一目标或理念，基于话语和视角的无限繁复？

We might want to say that parology, that the status of being an outsider to the majority or to the mainstream of discourse, is another way of talking about the condition which intellectuals sometimes call alienation.

我们或许可以说，谬误推理，即处于多数或主流话语之外的身份状态，是另一种谈论知识分子有时所称的“异化”状况的方式。

And if Lyotard wishes us to pursue parology as an end in itself, I think that what he's telling us is that alienation is an intrinsically good state of affairs, and that any real intellectual has to be alienated, elsewise you're being coerced by the dominant regime of discourse. So to be really free is to be constantly negating, constantly saying no, constantly saying, no, I don't think so, I'm not willing to accept that.

如果利奥塔希望我们将谬误推理本身作为目的来追求，我认为他是在告诉我们，异化本质上是一种好的状态，任何真正的知识分子都必须处于异化状态，否则你就会被主流话语体制所胁迫。因此，真正的自由就是不断地否定，不断地说“不”，不断地说：“不，我不这么认为，我不愿意接受那个。”

In other words, it means that naysaying, negation, rejection, it means that undermining of established verities and suspicion has now become an end in itself. It's not a means towards any substantial reality. Now it just gives us something to do. So we really are gesturing in the void, gesturing in the dark and at the dark towards God knows what for God knows what reason, because there are no reasons to give.

换句话说，这意味着说“不”、否定、拒绝，意味着对既定真理的颠覆和怀疑，如今已成为目的本身。它不再是通往任何实质现实的途径。现在它只是让我们有事可做。所以我们确实是在虚空中比划，在黑暗中向着黑暗比划，向着天知道什么、为了天知道什么理由，因为没有任何理由可给。

And here we end up in a position where we're drifting in the direction of Barthes, we're drifting in the direction of talking to ourselves, right? And not even that, because it means that the rules of our language game will change not just from person to person, but over time. My language games now are very different from my language games when I was five.

在这里，我们最终陷入一种境地：我们正漂向巴特的方向，漂向自言自语的方向，对吧？甚至还不止于此，因为这意味着我们语言游戏的规则不仅会因人而异，而且会随时间变化。我现在玩的语言游戏与我五岁时的语言游戏大不相同。

But there's no real improvement. I mean, I adopted one set of linguistic rules then, and I adopted another set now. And you can't say that one is better than the other. It's just different. It is an exceedingly democratic and exceedingly self-reassuring ideology because it means that everybody's right. And it's basically impossible to be mistaken. Except, of course, if you're a modernist. In which case it is possible. Although he never exactly explains how. Or what it would mean to be mistaken. Or how modernists could possibly...

但并没有真正的进步。我的意思是，我当时采用了一套语言规则，现在又采用了另一套。你不能说一套比另一套更好。只是不同而已。这是一种极其民主、极其自我安慰的意识形态，因为它意味着每个人都是对的，而且基本上不可能出错。当然，除非你是一个现代主义者。在这种情况下，出错是可能的。尽管他从未确切解释过如何出错，或者出错意味着什么，或者现代主义者怎么可能……

be in some condition of misapprehension. If postmodernity is right, it seems to me that this criticism of totalizing meta-discourses loses all explanatory force. It's one of the great difficulties that's going to emerge here. And I think we should look at our, at Lyotard as being a postmodern gamester, because there's a very playful element to this activity of criticizing everything just in the name of criticizing things and just in the name of creating dissenses which he thinks is just intrinsically good. The more alienated and angry and separate and apart intellectuals are from the dominant mainstream culture and from each other, the more individual language games there will be and the more quasi-communication or miscommunication or non-communication or whatever it is that goes on now in lieu of communication, there will be. So instead of going back to one universal paradigm of knowledge which made possible clear communication, now we'll go to an infinite plethora of models of knowledge which leaves the status of communication in great doubt.

……处于某种误解状态。如果后现代性是正确的，在我看来，这种对总体化元话语的批评就失去了所有解释力。这是这里将要出现的一个重大困难。我认为我们应该把利奥塔看作一个后现代的博弈者，因为这种仅仅以批评为名、以制造歧见为名的活动，带有很强的游戏性，他认为歧见本身就是好的。知识分子越是异化、愤怒、分离，越是远离主流文化和彼此，就会有越多的个体语言游戏，也会有更多的准交流、误交流、非交流，或者任何现在取代交流而进行的东西。因此，我们不会回到一个使清晰交流成为可能的普遍知识范式，而是会走向无限多样的知识模式，这使得交流的地位变得非常可疑。

There's a whole series of problems connected with Lyotard's post-modernity. Let's start with the beginning. The first and biggest problem here is that if real or if accurate or satisfactory or just discourse, you almost don't know what to say about this idea of paralogy, if that is the way we should approach science and knowledge and thought and legitimation, If there is an infinite number of language games with an infinite number of infinitely complex rules, each of which is separate to every individual, it generates the question of how is, and to what extent, is communication possible at all? Aren't we all playing our own language game? And don't these language games change at every instant in time? In which case, aren't we just making noise? Doesn't this just change all of intellectual discourse into scat singing?

与利奥塔的后现代性相关的问题有一系列。让我们从头开始。这里第一个也是最大的问题是，如果真实的、准确的、令人满意的或公正的话语——你几乎不知道该对这种谬误推理的理念说什么——如果这就是我们处理科学、知识、思想和合法化的方式，如果有无限数量的语言游戏，每个游戏都有无限复杂的规则，且每个规则对每个个体都是独立的，那么就会产生一个问题：交流究竟如何可能，以及在多大程度上可能？我们难道不都在玩自己的语言游戏吗？这些语言游戏难道不是每时每刻都在变化吗？如果是这样，我们难道不只是在制造噪音吗？这难道不是把所有的智识话语都变成了拟声吟唱吗？

### 9. The Differend and Its Examples (歧异及其例证)

Only the people who don't understand what scat singing is would actually ask, well, what do the lyrics mean? The lyrics don't mean anything, it turns out. It's just a way of making noise and gesturing at the domain of your perspective. And here we see it folding into that position of barth, that interiority, that retreat to within the self. And I think that there's a tendency, not just in leotard, but in all of post-modernity, to reject the external world because it gets in the way of our egocentrism. So the idea that we can no longer have totalizing meta-discourses and instead we're stuck with an infinite plurality of language games, it seems to me undermines the possibility of communication at all.

只有那些不懂拟声吟唱是什么的人才会问：“歌词是什么意思？”结果发现，歌词没有任何意义。这只是一种制造噪音、指向你自身视角领域的方式。在这里，我们看到它融入了巴特的那种立场，那种内在性，那种退回到自我内部的倾向。我认为，不仅在利奥塔那里，而且在所有后现代主义中，都有一种拒绝外部世界的倾向，因为外部世界妨碍了我们的自我中心主义。因此，认为我们不能再有总体化的元话语，而是被困在无限多元的语言游戏中的想法，在我看来，破坏了交流的可能性。

And it makes it very hard to be sure what we're talking about. And Lyotard doesn't make it any easier. He says that justice is still an important value, even though consensus is not, but never tells us what justice is. And not only that, but it does beg the question of, well, not only what do you mean by justice, but is your justice the same as mine? One assumes not. So why do I have to pursue your justice? What's it to me? I have my own little discourse. I don't need your help. I can be confused on my own. Right? So this activity of subjectivizing and radical relativism for both knowledge and speech and language ends up moving perilously close to solipsism and making communication impossible altogether.

这使得我们很难确定我们在谈论什么。而利奥塔并没有让事情变得更容易。他说正义仍然是一个重要的价值，尽管共识不是，但他从未告诉我们正义是什么。不仅如此，这还引出了一个问题：不仅你所说的正义是什么意思，而且你的正义和我的正义是一样的吗？大概不一样。那么我为什么要追求你的正义？这关我什么事？我有我自己的小话语。我不需要你的帮助。我自己也能困惑。对吧？因此，这种对知识、言语和语言的主观化和激进相对主义活动，最终危险地接近唯我论，并使交流完全不可能。

It's a site, and here I mean in a metaphorical, not a literal sense, a site of non-communication between incommensurable and untranslatable discourses. And the problem with the Differand is that in the modernist conception of the world, there should be no such thing. If there is one big totalizing meta-discourse, then whenever people have a fundamental disagreement, an untranslatable conflict about the nature of the world, one of them has to be right, one of them has to be wrong, they can't both be right.

这是一个场所，我在此处指的是隐喻意义上的，而非字面意义上的，一个不可通约且不可翻译的话语之间无法沟通的场所。而歧感（Differend）的问题在于，在现代主义的世界观中，本不应存在这样的东西。如果存在一个宏大的总体化元话语，那么每当人们对世界的本质产生根本分歧、发生不可翻译的冲突时，其中一方必然正确，另一方必然错误，他们不可能都对。

Lyotard says no. The simple, brute fact of the world is that there is no totalizing meta-discourse. It's not the truth that one of them has to be right. Both of them can be right, or more precisely, it doesn't mean anything to describe either of them as being right or wrong. It's just what you like and what you don't like.

利奥塔说并非如此。这个世界简单而赤裸的事实是，不存在总体化的元话语。并非其中一方必然正确。双方都可能正确，或者更准确地说，将任何一方描述为正确或错误都没有意义。这只是你喜欢什么和不喜欢什么的问题。

Now, look at the way this is going to work, because when we look at the examples of it, it becomes a lot clearer. What he says is, look, there are certain systems of signs, certain ways of describing and organizing the world and articulating the world, which prejudge the answers to the questions it is going to ask, and which are incommensurate and untranslatable with certain other discourses, which claim to be describing the same thing. And this leads us to certain verbal impasses. And what it means is that Plato was wrong, the dialectic doesn't lead to truth, it leads to a dead end. It leads to people yelling at each other and then beating each other up. Take an example from contemporary politics, abortion. There's a group of people who are members of a linguistic community who regard the legality of abortion as a question of killing babies.

现在，看看这将如何运作，因为当我们审视其例证时，事情会变得清晰得多。他说的是，看，有些符号系统、某些描述和组织世界、表达世界的方式，它们预先判断了将要提出的问题的答案，并且与某些声称描述同一事物的其他话语不可通约、不可翻译。这导致我们陷入某些言语僵局。这意味着柏拉图错了，辩证法并不通向真理，而是通向死胡同。它导致人们互相叫嚷，然后互相殴打。以当代政治中的一个例子为例：堕胎。有一群人，他们属于某个语言共同体，将堕胎的合法性视为杀害婴儿的问题。

There is another group of people, a separate community of discourse within the society, that regards the legality of abortion as being a question of personal freedom and personal choice. Now the question is, from the modernist, I guess, what's the real answer? Well, from the postmodern perspective, there's just no real answer. It's not that one side is right and the other side is wrong because they've adopted the right perspective. What you have here is two untranslatable and incommensurate discourses constructed by people that just cannot talk to each other. In other words, and if you formulate your question about abortion within the confines of their linguistic system, within the confines of their discourse, you will have already presupposed the answer. In other words, there is no neutral middle ground in language where we can dispassionately and objectively search for knowledge. There's just an infinite set of perspectives.

还有另一群人，社会中一个独立的话语共同体，将堕胎的合法性视为个人自由和个人选择的问题。那么问题是，从现代主义的角度来看，我想，真正的答案是什么？嗯，从后现代的角度来看，根本没有真正的答案。并非一方正确而另一方错误，因为他们采用了正确的视角。这里有的是两种不可翻译、不可通约的话语，由那些根本无法相互交谈的人构建而成。换句话说，如果你在他们的语言系统范围内、在他们的话语范围内阐述你关于堕胎的问题，你便已经预设了答案。换言之，在语言中不存在一个中立的中立地带，让我们可以冷静而客观地寻求知识。存在的只是无穷无尽的视角。

So what this means then is that abortion is a good thing if you say Is freedom of choice for women a good thing? Well, it presupposes that the answer is going to be yes. If you're going to ask, is killing babies a good thing? You're presupposing that the answer is no. How do you translate those two propositions with both gesturing at the same fact of social reality? How do we find some neutral middle ground where we're not presupposing that? There's Lyotard's point, and it's a deep point. We don't know how to do it.

因此，这意味着，如果你问“女性的选择自由是好事吗？”，那么堕胎就是好事。这预设了答案是肯定的。如果你要问“杀害婴儿是好事吗？”，你预设了答案是否定的。你如何翻译这两种命题，而它们都指向同一社会现实事实？我们如何找到某种不预设答案的中立地带？这就是利奥塔的观点，而且是一个深刻的观点。我们不知道如何做到这一点。

And Lyotard has drawn the somewhat disconcerting inference that perhaps there's no way to do it, and perhaps the longing to do it is an atavistic holdover from our modernist totalizing discourses. Perhaps there's just no such thing. And not only that, perhaps the fact that we can't achieve a consensus about abortion or a great many other matters is a good thing, because that's what ensures freedom, that's what ensures a plurality of possible ways of looking at the world, and that's what prevents...

利奥塔得出了一个有些令人不安的推论：也许根本无法做到这一点，也许对这种做法的渴望是我们现代主义总体化话语中残留的返祖现象。也许根本没有这种东西。不仅如此，也许我们无法就堕胎或许多其他问题达成共识这一事实是件好事，因为那正是确保自由、确保看待世界的多种可能方式的东西，而且那正是防止……

Totalizing discourse, or what that is, what prevents discourse from becoming totalitarian. We want to let a hundred flowers bloom, and if you were able to reduce them all to one universal question-and-answer format where you get substantial reality, that makes democracy superfluous. So he says then that the only way to get beyond this is to construct a fictitious, mythological totalizing discourse. If you don't want to do that, then parology is here to stay. It's not something he recently invented. He's just found a way of valorizing it and saying that he likes it, of affirming it. But it's been around all the time. It's been around since the beginning of time. A very interesting kind of an argument. And you might want to go back and look at something like, say, the conflict between, I don't know, Plato or Socrates and any of the sophists that he talks to in the dialogues. It's parology. They're based upon different assumptions about the world, and they're talking past each other.

总体化话语，或者说，那是什么，是什么阻止话语变成极权主义。我们要让百花齐放，如果你能把它们全部归结为一种普遍的问答形式，从而获得实质性的现实，那就会使民主变得多余。因此他说，超越这一点的唯一方法是构建一种虚构的、神话般的总体化话语。如果你不想这样做，那么谬误推理（parology）就会继续存在。这不是他最近发明的。他只是找到了一种方式来推崇它，说喜欢它，肯定它。但它一直存在。它从时间的开端就存在了。这是一种非常有趣的论证。你或许可以回头看看，比如柏拉图或苏格拉底与他在对话中交谈的任何智者之间的冲突。那就是谬误推理。它们基于对世界的不同假设，彼此各说各话。

### 10. Just Gaming, Terror, and Critiques of Postmodernism (公正游戏、恐怖与对后现代主义的批判)

There's a deep fact about social reality here, that although we may long for some ultimate coherence to our discourses, we don't know what that is, and we don't have very good grounds for believing in such a thing. It's an act of faith rather than an act of knowledge. Another question, another problem that emerges. This emerges in a wonderful book that he wrote called Just Gaming, which shows how playful, how Wittgensteinian it is. The obvious reference is to Wittgenstein and the idea of language games. But what's nice about the English translation, because the French was called en justice, just gaming means just in the sense of only gaming, merely gaming, and also...

这里有一个关于社会现实的深刻事实：尽管我们可能渴望我们的话语具有某种终极一致性，但我们不知道那是什么，而且我们没有很好的理由相信这样的东西存在。这是一种信仰行为，而非知识行为。另一个问题，另一个浮现的问题。这出现在他写的一本精彩著作《公正游戏》（Just Gaming）中，这本书展示了它是多么有趣、多么维特根斯坦式。明显的参照是维特根斯坦和语言游戏的概念。但英文翻译的妙处在于，因为法文原版名为“en justice”，just gaming 意味着“仅仅是游戏”、“只是游戏”，同时也意味着……

Fairly or justly or righteously gaming. So it's a lovely play on words and of course this is the strong point of Pomo writers, right? Plays on words, puns, ambiguities, that's their strong point. So just gaming is a wonderful, almost a better title than the original, the French original. And what we find out in Just Gaming is he wants to investigate the question of is it possible to use parology and the idea of keeping the different alive, not silencing terroristically the different, is it possible to use that as the source of a new legitimation for paralogical postmodern thought? Well...

“公正地”、“正义地”或“正当地”游戏。所以这是一个可爱的文字游戏，当然这是后现代作家的强项，对吧？文字游戏、双关语、歧义，那是他们的强项。所以《公正游戏》是一个绝妙的标题，几乎比法文原版更好。而在《公正游戏》中，我们发现他想探讨的问题是：是否有可能利用谬误推理和保持歧感（differend）鲜活、不让歧感被恐怖地压制这一理念，作为后现代谬误推理思想的新合法化来源？那么……

It's a beautiful little discourse.
