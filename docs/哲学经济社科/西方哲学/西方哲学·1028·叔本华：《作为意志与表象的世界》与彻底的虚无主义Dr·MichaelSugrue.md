Now, the problem that Hume ran into is that although we see tables and chairs and people and floors and ceilings, we never see certain things which we think of are important parts of the world. For example, Hume will tell us that you never see time.

那么让我们从头开始。对于休谟和英国经验主义者来说，心灵是他们所谓的 tabula rasa，意思是白板。另一种思考方式是，心灵是一个空盒子。婴儿出生后开始看、听、触摸、品尝，感觉进入那个空盒子。然后，心灵在使这些感觉连贯的活动中，将它们捆绑在一起。例如，如果我看这个讲台，我会看到它是硬的，有一定重量，是光滑的，是棕色的。我所做的是将这些感觉捆绑在一起，形成称为讲台的东西。这有一定道理。现在，休谟遇到的问题是这样的：尽管我们看到桌子、椅子、人、地板和天花板，但我们从未看到某些我们认为世界重要部分的东西。例如，休谟会告诉我们，你从未看到时间。

You never see space. You never see causality. And obviously that's true. If I were to drop this pen, take a pen and drop it, we see the causal relation. We see me pick the pen up, let it go, and it falls. And if I do that a hundred times, we'll see it fall every time. Hume's point will be, though, although we see the event of me holding the pen up and the pen falling, we never see causality. All we see is a regularity between the two. Similarly, if you look around for time, you're going to look a long time. You may look at clocks, you may look at calendars, but all you're going to see are pieces of paper and mechanisms. You're not going to see time. So what Hume decides is that it's not there. It's a sort of imaginary thing, the way we glue the universe together.

你从未看到空间。你从未看到因果性。这显然是真实的。如果我拿起这支笔，然后松开它，我们看到因果关系。我们看到我拿起笔，松开它，它落下。如果我这样做一百次，我们会看到它每次都落下。但休谟的观点是，尽管我们看到我握住笔和笔落下的事件，我们从未看到因果性。我们看到的只是两者之间的规律性。同样，如果你环顾四周寻找时间，你会找很长时间。你可能会看时钟，看日历，但你看到的只是纸和机械装置。你不会看到时间。所以休谟断定它不在那里。它是一种想象的东西，是我们将宇宙粘合在一起的方式。

And Kant rejects this kind of skepticism. In other words, we have a problem here. We don't know how it is we found it about space and time and causality and being and negation and number and all the things which empiricism really lacks a good coherent account for. Kant takes up that challenge. What Kant does is says, no, David Hume, you are wrong. In fact, the human mind is not a blank slate. It is not a tabula rasa. In fact, the human mind doesn't passively absorb sensation, rather it actively constructs the external world. And when you miss that activity of the human mind in constructing the external world, you cause yourself all kinds of philosophical paradoxes. It's a brilliant answer. See if I can explain it. For Kant, think of the human mind as being a room, like this room. And say that the room has one window.

而康德拒绝这种怀疑论。换句话说，我们这里有一个问题。我们不知道我们是如何发现空间、时间、因果性、存在、否定、数字以及所有经验主义缺乏连贯解释的事物的。康德接受了这一挑战。康德所做的是说，不，大卫·休谟，你错了。事实上，人类心灵不是一块白板。它不是 tabula rasa。事实上，人类心灵不是被动地吸收感觉，而是主动地建构外部世界。当你忽略了人类心灵在建构外部世界中的这种活动时，你会给自己带来各种哲学悖论。这是一个精彩的回答。看看我能否解释它。对于康德来说，把人类心灵想象成一个房间，就像这个房间。假设这个房间有一扇窗户。

And only one window. And the doors are locked. You're never going to get outside this room. That's the human mind itself. Now, when you look at the external world, when you observe and experience external reality, it looks to you like external reality is shaped exactly like the window. Of course it is. Well, because that's your only access to it. Literally speaking, the shape of the window, as far as you're concerned, is the shape of what's outside the window. In other words, the light that comes in is precisely shaped by the opening that lets it in. In other words, there's a form that the mind imposes on external experience. And this activity of imposing these forms is a far better, more accurate, truer representation of human cognition. So instead of being a blank slate or an empty box, it's actually actively constructing the external world.

而且只有一扇窗户。门是锁着的。你永远无法离开这个房间。那就是人类心灵本身。现在，当你观察外部世界，当你观察和体验外部现实时，对你来说，外部现实的形状似乎恰好与窗户相同。当然是这样。因为那是你接触它的唯一途径。从字面上说，窗户的形状，就你而言，就是窗外事物的形状。换句话说，进入的光线恰好被让光线进入的开口所塑造。换句话说，心灵将某种形式强加于外部经验之上。而这种强加形式的活动是对人类认知的一种更好、更准确、更真实的表征。因此，心灵不是一块白板或一个空盒子，而是实际上在主动建构外部世界。

Let me give you an example from the podium. I experience the podium, for example, as being both spatial and temporal. It exists in time. It was here when I started the lecture, and it'll be here when I finish, with any luck. And it takes up a certain space. If I walk into that space, I'm going to hit the podium. Now, what Kant says is that the mind contains, I believe it's 12, a priori forms of human cognition. A priori means that it's prior to our experience of the world. In other words, it's built in to experiencing the world as a human subject. And these a priori forms necessarily shape the way we experience the external world. So Kant says that the external world is composed of...

让我从讲台举个例子。例如，我体验这个讲台，既是空间的又是时间的。它存在于时间中。我开始讲座时它就在这里，如果运气好的话，我讲完时它还会在这里。而且它占据一定的空间。如果我走进那个空间，我会撞上讲台。现在，康德所说的是，心智包含——我相信是12个——人类认知的先验形式。先验意味着它先于我们对世界的经验。换句话说，它是作为人类主体体验世界所固有的。这些先验形式必然塑造我们体验外部世界的方式。所以康德说，外部世界是由……组成的

Dingen an sich, what that means is things in themselves. So there are podiums, there are cups of coffee, there are pieces of paper, there are people and there are objects in the world, but we can never experience these objects in an unmediated way, the way Hume assumes. Actually, we actively construct these objects by imposing on them categories we can't avoid. Space, time, causality, number, being, negation, and this is built into experiencing the world as a subject. This is all, this thousands of pages of philosophy are encapsulated in a line from T.S. Eliot in the Four Quartets where he says, he's in the rose garden, he says, the roses had the look of flowers that are looked at.

Dingen an sich，意思是“物自体”。所以世界上有讲台、咖啡杯、纸张、人和物体，但我们永远无法像休谟假设的那样，以无中介的方式体验这些物体。实际上，我们通过强加给我们无法避免的范畴来主动建构这些物体：空间、时间、因果性、数量、存在、否定，这是作为主体体验世界所固有的。所有这些，这数千页的哲学，都浓缩在T.S.艾略特《四个四重奏》中的一行诗里，他在玫瑰园中说：“玫瑰有着被注视的花朵的模样。”

Isn't that beautiful? That just gets it all together. Well, the podium has the look of a podium that is looked at. It's not like this in and of itself. We can't find out what the podium is in and of itself. We have to experience the world through space, time, causality, through these necessary forms. That's why we all know what space is, but nobody can explain it to you. If somebody really said they didn't know what quantity was, what would we do? We all know what quantity is, we just can't explain it. It's built right into the hard wiring of the human psyche. It's a deep answer, a very important answer.

这难道不美吗？这真是把一切都凝聚在一起了。那么，讲台有着被注视的讲台的模样。它本身并非如此。我们无法发现讲台本身是什么。我们必须通过空间、时间、因果性，通过这些必然形式来体验世界。这就是为什么我们都知道空间是什么，但没有人能向你解释它。如果有人真的说他们不知道数量是什么，我们会怎么做？我们都知道数量是什么，只是无法解释。它直接内置于人类心理的硬接线中。这是一个深刻的答案，一个非常重要的答案。

### 5. The World as Representation and the Discovery of the Will (作为表征的世界与意志的发现)

And this is what Schopenhauer takes as his foundation. In other words, the first movement of the world as idea, the world as Vorstellung, the world as representation. In other words, he's saying that the external world is what Kant says it is. It's one big idea. So we have all these overlapping forms, and our whole experience of the world is one big idea. So in the first part of this, he's being a good Kantian, and he's saying that the whole phenomenal world is one big Vorstellung. The subject constructs the object.

这就是叔本华作为其基础的东西。换句话说，作为观念的世界、作为Vorstellung的世界、作为表征的世界的第一个运动。换句话说，他在说外部世界就是康德所说的那样。它是一个大观念。所以我们有所有这些重叠的形式，我们对世界的整个经验就是一个大观念。所以在第一部分，他是一个好的康德主义者，他说整个现象世界是一个大Vorstellung。主体建构客体。

It's an activity of the mind. Now what Kant says in addition to this, or it's correlative of this, is that we can't know the Ding-on-Zich. Ding-on-Zich means the thing in itself. I can't experience the podium as it really is, I guess in the mind of God, independent of space and time and causality and number. I have to look through the window. I have no choice. I have no access to it. But that means that we never experience the external world as it is. We experience the external world as a human being has to experience things. So far, so good. What that means is that we can never know these things in and of themselves. We never get direct access to noumena. We only have the phenomenal world which we construct through the a priori forms.

这是心智的一种活动。现在康德除此之外还说的，或者与之相关的，是我们无法认识Ding-an-sich。Ding-an-sich意思是物自体。我无法体验讲台的真实样子，我想在上帝的心智中，独立于空间、时间、因果性和数量。我必须透过窗户看。我别无选择。我无法直接接触它。但这意味着我们从未体验过外部世界的本来面目。我们体验外部世界的方式是作为人类必须体验事物的方式。到目前为止，一切顺利。这意味着我们永远无法认识这些事物本身。我们永远无法直接接触本体。我们只有通过先验形式建构的现象世界。

Now, in the second movement of Schopenhauer's work, what he says is, no, Kant is wrong. He's going to invert Kant and says, yes, there is one example of direct apprehension of a noumenal ding-on-zig. And the one thing that I can know about in this particular way, in this noumenal way, the one ding on zik that I can know is the will, my own self-consciousness. In other words, my own consciousness of myself as a self is not mediated by the a priori forms. I have direct, immediate apprehension of that fact. And this is noumenal. This is not phenomenal. This is the thing in itself.

现在，在叔本华著作的第二个运动中，他说，不，康德错了。他将颠倒康德，并说，是的，有一个直接把握本体性物自体的例子。我能以这种特殊方式、这种本体方式认识的唯一一件事就是意志，我自己的自我意识。换句话说，我对自己作为自我的意识并不受先验形式的中介。我对那个事实有直接的、即时的把握。这是本体的。这不是现象的。这是物自体。

So what he's saying then is that the outside world is just like Kant said it was, mediated by the a priori forms. The internal world, alas, is not the way Kant thought it was. It's one unitary thing, will. And we get direct, immediate apprehension of that. And this introduction of the idea of will into German philosophy, or an emphasis on will, is going to be pregnant with very important consequences for the late 19th and early 20th century, if you think of people like Nietzsche. This easily gets transformed into the will to power.

所以他所说的是，外部世界正如康德所说，受先验形式中介。内部世界，唉，并非康德所想的那样。它是一个统一的东西，意志。我们对它有直接、即时的把握。这种将意志观念引入德国哲学，或对意志的强调，将对19世纪末和20世纪初产生非常重要的后果，如果你想到尼采这样的人。这很容易转化为权力意志。

So what we're going to see here is that my knowledge of the internal world is of my own will. And what that means is that human beings, like Hobbes said, are desiring animals. We desire this, we desire that. But Schopenhauer will change that slightly and say, yes, we are desiring animals, but we do not know ourselves. We do not really know our own wills. Consciousness is only a subset of will. Reason is only a subset of will, and in fact, will is a force that reason neither controls nor understands. We are subject to a terrifying force, bigger than we are, mysterious to us, that we are unable to control. This will be a great prelude to Freud. If you think about the idea of the unconscious as being a part of our will, a part of our cogito, that we are unaware of, this begins to make a certain degree of sense.

所以我们在这里将看到的是，我对内部世界的知识是对我自己意志的知识。这意味着人类，如霍布斯所说，是欲望的动物。我们渴望这个，渴望那个。但叔本华会稍微改变这一点，说，是的，我们是欲望的动物，但我们不了解自己。我们并不真正了解我们自己的意志。意识只是意志的一个子集。理性只是意志的一个子集，事实上，意志是一种理性既不控制也不理解的力量。我们受制于一种可怕的力量，比我们更大，对我们来说神秘，我们无法控制。这将是弗洛伊德的伟大前奏。如果你想到无意识作为我们意志的一部分、我们cogito的一部分，而我们没有意识到，这就开始有了一定的意义。

So we lose that enlightenment optimism, the idea that we are reasonable animals in control of our wills. In fact, the will is controlling us, and the will is a mysterious force that we find not just in individual people, but in all of nature and in all of human beings as a whole. So it's one big unitary thing, and we can get knowledge of this noumenal ding-on-zik directly. Will is a blind, atelological force which animates all of nature. It would be worth comparing this to Spinoza's idea of canatus, the desire of all things to continue being what they are.

所以我们失去了启蒙运动的乐观主义，即我们是理性的动物，控制着我们的意志。事实上，意志在控制我们，而意志是一种神秘的力量，我们不仅在个体身上发现，而且在所有自然和所有人类整体中发现。所以它是一个统一的东西，我们可以直接获得对这个本体性物自体的知识。意志是一种盲目的、无目的的力量，它赋予所有自然以活力。这值得与斯宾诺莎的conatus概念相比较，即所有事物持续存在其本性的欲望。

### 6. Pessimism and the Human Psyche (悲观主义与人类心理)

And it's a sort of inversion of that. All things would like to be something else, but they really don't have any control of what they are, so they kind of muddle along as best they can. It's an extremely sort of pessimistic view of the human psyche. We play things in the hands of not even God, but something less than God because it doesn't have a will and doesn't like us. It just is what it is. It's a sort of terrifying view of human beings as being kind of froth on the ocean of reality.

而且这有点像是那种观点的反转。万物都想成为别的什么，但它们实际上无法控制自己是什么，所以它们只能尽力凑合着过。这是一种对人类心理极其悲观的观点。我们被玩弄于股掌之中，甚至不是上帝之手，而是某种不如上帝的东西，因为它没有意志，也不喜欢我们。它只是它本来的样子。这是一种可怕的观点，认为人类不过是现实海洋上的泡沫。

Now, this is a problem because in some ways it's anti-Socratic. It means essentially that you can't know yourself or that if you can know yourself, it's only after the fact. 现在，这是个问题，因为从某种意义上说，这是反苏格拉底的。它本质上意味着你无法认识自己，或者即使你能认识自己，也只能在事后。换句话说，你只有在发现自己真正做了什么之后，才能发现自己真正想要什么。这再次与弗洛伊德联系起来。为什么我忘了那个人的名字？因为你其实不喜欢他们，你其实不想和他们说话。嗯，我的意思是，这在叔本华的作品中已有预示。你后来回想起来，说，我希望我记得那个人的名字。然后你意识到，你并不想记得。这就是为什么你没记住。所以这里有一种关于失误动作的理论，我认为弗洛伊德将会采纳它。

So what do we get? We get the world as outside and the world as inside. The world as outside is the world as idea. The world as inside is the world as will. And this faces us with a problem. What the hell are we? And how are we to deal with all these difficulties that the world confronts us with? Because we are desiring animals, and desire is a purely negative condition. Here comes the Buddhism. Here comes the Hinduism. Here comes that kind of pessimistic Eastern philosophy. Desire is a problem. And the only sensible thing to do is to try and get away from desire. 那么我们得到了什么？我们得到了外部世界和内部世界。外部世界是作为表象的世界。内部世界是作为意志的世界。而这让我们面临一个问题：我们到底是什么？我们该如何应对世界向我们提出的所有这些困难？因为我们是欲望的动物，而欲望纯粹是一种否定状态。佛教来了，印度教来了，那种悲观的东方哲学来了。欲望是个问题。唯一明智的做法是努力摆脱欲望。而人类生活是一种匮乏。我们最好根本不存在。

You're better off not feeling anything. You're better off not existing at all.

但因为我们存在，我们必须从一个欲望跌跌撞撞到另一个欲望，从未真正控制它们，甚至我们欲望的满足也是纯粹否定的。换句话说，满足你对食物、性、爱或任何东西的欲望，就像挠痒痒。挠的时候很舒服，但你不痒会更好。你什么感觉都没有会更好。你根本不存在会更好。

But alas, you do exist. And so what are we supposed to do with this mess of a world? You can see the connection between this and Buddha's eight noble truths. Suffering, death, disease. This is what the human condition is. We long for nirvana. We long for the abolition of the self. We long essentially for death. This is cheery. Do you see why this is described as a pessimism? It would be hard to think of anything more relentlessly negative than Schopenhauer.

但可惜，你确实存在。那么我们该如何处理这个混乱的世界呢？你可以看到这与佛陀的八正道之间的联系。痛苦、死亡、疾病。这就是人类的境况。我们渴望涅槃，渴望自我的消亡，我们本质上渴望死亡。这真是令人振奋。你明白为什么这被称为悲观主义了吧？很难想象还有比叔本华更彻底消极的东西了。

So we have the dubious possibility of self-knowledge. We have the problem of the self. It is a spiritual problem, and alas, it has no spiritual solution, which is truly pathetic. And the last two movements of this great philosophical symphony, this kind of exercise in negativity, are an attempt to solve the problem of the world as idea in movement three, and then movement four, it's an attempt to solve the problem of the world as will.

所以我们有可疑的自我认识的可能性。我们有自我问题。这是一个精神问题，可惜没有精神上的解决方案，这真是可悲。这部伟大的哲学交响曲的最后两个乐章，这种否定性的练习，分别是试图解决作为表象的世界的问题（第三乐章），然后第四乐章试图解决作为意志的世界的问题。

### 7. Aesthetics as Escape: Art, Tragedy, and Music (作为逃避的美学：艺术、悲剧与音乐)

Let's look at the third section, solution to the problem of noumena, the solution to the problem of the world as idea. How can we escape from this prison of subjectivity that we are locked into by the Kantian categories? How will we be something other than purely contingent, purely subjective beings, aching for release from this veil of maya, this continuous parade of desires that we neither need nor gratify?

让我们看看第三部分，对本体的问题的解决，对作为表象的世界的问题的解决。我们如何逃离这个被康德范畴锁定的主观性监狱？我们如何成为不仅仅是纯粹偶然的、纯粹主观的存在，渴望从摩耶之幕中解脱，从这场我们既不需要也不满足的连续欲望游行中解脱？

Well, there are two ways out, according to Schopenhauer, and I think this is the greatest contribution of his book, to be honest. In other words, this has certainly been the most influential part. First way out is through aesthetics. Art offers us a sort of redemption. As Schopenhauer said, artistic aesthetic satisfaction, aesthetic apprehension, is the purest kind of objectivity.

嗯，根据叔本华的说法，有两条出路，我认为这是他这本书最大的贡献，说实话。换句话说，这肯定是最有影响力的部分。第一条出路是通过美学。艺术为我们提供了一种救赎。正如叔本华所说，艺术的审美满足、审美领悟是最纯粹的客观性。

And there's a certain plausibility to that. In other words, I'm not convinced that that's wrong. That may be the case, and it certainly has been very influential in the history of aesthetics. It's often the case that when philosophers give up on the idea of theology, when they give up on the idea of moral order to the world, the second best or next best alternative is to look for some sort of ersatz transcendence in the realm of art.
