And for that reason, what we call truth is in fact the errors we haven't refuted yet. It means that the drive towards knowledge that we've gotten from both Greece and from Christianity is now being undermined and being devalued.

我们所能达到的最好的知识，既然上帝不再在场来验证我们的知识，柏拉图发明的形式领域也不再在场来验证我们的知识，我们所能得到的最好的东西，不过是一系列关于世界的视角。因此，我们所谓的真理，实际上是我们尚未反驳的错误。这意味着，我们从希腊和基督教那里继承而来的对知识的追求，现在正被削弱和贬低。

### 4. Revaluation of Values and Self-Image / Personal Reading and the Gay Science / Oxymoron as Key and Attack over Defense / Exceptional Men and Herd Morality (价值重估与自我形象 / 个人阅读与《快乐的科学》 / 矛盾修辞作为关键与进攻而非防御 / 杰出人物与群氓道德)

Part of Nietzsche's program, he calls the revaluation of all values, changing the entire tablet of good and evil that had been handed down from generation to generation throughout the Western tradition. He says, I'm going to destroy that tablet and construct a new one. He intends to replace the tradition of Western religion with his own thought. He also at the same time intends to abolish the tradition of Western philosophy because he thinks that it needs metaphysics and once he gets rid of metaphysics then there will be no more tradition of Western philosophy. In other words, he seriously and literally and honestly thinks that Western philosophy and religion end with him.

尼采计划的一部分，他称之为“一切价值的重估”，即改变西方传统中代代相传的善恶牌匾。他说，我将摧毁那块牌匾，并建造一块新的。他打算用自己的思想取代西方宗教的传统。同时，他也打算废除西方哲学的传统，因为他认为哲学需要形而上学，而一旦他摆脱了形而上学，就不会再有西方哲学的传统了。换句话说，他严肃、字面且诚实地认为，西方哲学和宗教在他这里终结。

Now this is no small estimation of oneself, right? And for a man who in print refers to himself as the Antichrist, you may perhaps get the idea that megalomania has set in somewhere along the line here, and in fact, there's a considerable justice in that sort of a criticism. On the one hand, he is dealing with the greatest of philosophical problems, and in all honesty and in fairness, he is a superior man, a man of superior intellectual merit, and a man of great courage. It is not an easy, it's easy to ignore a religion. It is very hard to abolish it.

这可不是对自己的小小评价，对吧？对于一个在出版物中自称“反基督者”的人来说，你或许会想到，自大狂在某个地方已经出现了。事实上，这种批评有相当大的合理性。一方面，他在处理最伟大的哲学问题，而且公平地说，他是一个卓越的人，一个具有卓越智力价值的人，一个非常勇敢的人。忽视一种宗教是容易的，但废除它却非常困难。

They're quite different. It is one thing to be indifferent to religious questions and another thing to decide to torch them all. This is a very different set of expectations. He is not at all a prosaic writer and no one reads his books without one way or another being affected. I remember in particular when I first read The Gay Science and I read the passage about God being dead. I had to read it again and again and again. It was one of the most difficult passages I read when I was in college, and to this day I have never completely made my peace with it. It will repay a very close reading on your part if you ever get to the gay science.

这两者截然不同。对宗教问题漠不关心是一回事，决定将它们付之一炬则是另一回事。这是完全不同的期望。他绝不是一个平淡无奇的作家，没有人读他的书而不以某种方式受到影响。我特别记得，当我第一次读《快乐的科学》，读到关于上帝之死的段落时，我不得不一遍又一遍地重读。那是我大学时读过的最难懂的段落之一，直到今天我从未完全与之和解。如果你有机会读到《快乐的科学》，它值得你非常仔细地阅读。

In the process of reading his works, The Gay Science and the Others, always keep in mind this idea of oxymoron. It will make your life so much easier. Instead of trying to tease out some sense out of irrefutable errors, remember that this is Nietzsche's stock in trade. This is what he's good at because it gives you something provocative to think about without committing him to any logical position. If you go back and try and figure out exactly what Nietzsche does believe, it is not entirely easy to put together. Like many other philosophers, he is better at explaining, or if not explaining, attacking the things he does not like than in articulating a defense for the things that he does approve of. He does say that ethics, because it had been previously based upon Christian religion or because it had been based upon metaphysical philosophy, that ethics is over in the sense that the traditions of right and wrong that had been handed down for the previous

在阅读他的作品（《快乐的科学》及其他）的过程中，始终牢记矛盾修辞这个理念。它将使你的生活轻松得多。与其试图从“不可反驳的错误”中梳理出某种意义，不如记住这是尼采的惯用伎俩。这是他擅长的，因为它给你一些挑衅性的思考，而无需让他承诺任何逻辑立场。如果你回头试图弄清楚尼采到底相信什么，你会发现并不完全容易拼凑起来。像许多其他哲学家一样，他更擅长解释，或者如果不是解释，则是攻击他不喜欢的东西，而不是为他不赞成的东西阐述辩护。他确实说过，伦理学，由于它以前基于基督教宗教或基于形而上学的哲学，伦理学已经终结，因为先前西方15或20或25个世纪传下来的对错传统现在已经消失了。

15 or 20 or 25 centuries in the West are now gone. Ethics turns out to be one of these irrefutable errors. And it would seem that for an error that's irrefutable, he is trying very hard to refute it. Remember, though, that Nietzsche, because he is not a philosopher, because he thinks philosophy is over, and is primarily a poet, not a logician, doesn't care if he contradicts himself. Remember that the essence of an oxymoron is a contradictory utterance. Hot fire or hot ice doesn't make any sense. Cold fire doesn't make sense, but it is a very moving kind of locution that pushes you in a poetic rather than a logical way. This is what Nietzsche is good at.

伦理学结果证明是这些不可反驳的错误之一。而且看起来，对于一个不可反驳的错误，他却在非常努力地反驳它。但请记住，尼采，因为他不是哲学家，因为他认为哲学已经终结，而且他主要是一个诗人，而不是逻辑学家，所以不在乎自己是否自相矛盾。记住，矛盾修辞的本质是矛盾的陈述。“炽热的火焰”或“炽热的冰”没有意义。“冰冷的火焰”没有意义，但它是一种非常动人的措辞，以诗意而非逻辑的方式推动你。这就是尼采擅长的。

Now, in abolishing morality, in destroying ethics, he is taking a position very much like that of Raskolnikov in Dostoevsky's great novel Crime and Punishment. If any of you know that piece, you will know that early in the novel, before Raskolnikov comes back to the Christian fold, he commits a murder because he has the theory of the exceptional man, that the usual norms of right and wrong, which we expect to be universal, are only partially complete. There are certain superior individuals, according to Raskolnikov, for whom the traditions of morality do not apply. That's the idea of the exceptional man. Well, Nietzsche, in fact, makes an argument along these lines, that the great world historical individuals, that superior men, do not have to be bound by the constraints of traditional notions of right and wrong. They are the great artists. They are the great creators. And they may be artists in the sense of painters, artists in the sense of literary men.

现在，在废除道德、摧毁伦理的过程中，他所采取的立场与陀思妥耶夫斯基的伟大小说《罪与罚》中的拉斯柯尔尼科夫非常相似。如果你们中有人知道这部作品，就会知道在小说的开头，在拉斯柯尔尼科夫回归基督教信仰之前，他犯下了一桩谋杀，因为他持有一种“非凡之人”的理论，即我们通常期望具有普遍性的对错规范，其实只是部分完整的。根据拉斯柯尔尼科夫的观点，有些优越的个体，对他们而言，道德传统并不适用。这就是“非凡之人”的观念。尼采实际上也提出了类似的论证，认为那些伟大的世界历史人物，那些优越的人，不必受制于传统对错观念的约束。他们是伟大的艺术家，是伟大的创造者。他们可能是画家意义上的艺术家，也可能是文人意义上的艺术家。

But they are creative in things like politics or things like science as well. From this perspective, from the perspective of Nietzsche, all superior men in every discipline that are breaking the bounds of inherited forms, they are what he means by an artist. The artist is the creative human being. And the creative human being can create in any domain. It doesn't have to be a sculpture or literature or something like that. What he is saying is that that art and that capacity to create is more important than any moral judgment we might make about an artist. An artist may be a wicked man, but if he writes well, then he is a superior individual. It doesn't make any difference whether he obeys the usual rules that we like to impose upon all people. What makes a man great is his willingness to give up that herd morality as Nietzsche calls it.

但他们同样在政治或科学等领域具有创造性。从这个角度来看，从尼采的视角来看，所有在各自学科中打破既有形式束缚的优越之人，都是他所说的“艺术家”。艺术家就是具有创造力的人。而具有创造力的人可以在任何领域进行创造，不一定非得是雕塑、文学或类似的东西。他要说的是，艺术和那种创造能力比我们对艺术家可能做出的任何道德判断都更为重要。一个艺术家可能是个邪恶的人，但如果他写得好，那么他就是个优越的个体。他是否遵守我们通常希望强加于所有人的规则，这并不重要。使一个人伟大的，是他愿意放弃尼采所称的“群畜道德”。

### 5. Christianity as Slave Morality and Critique of Equality / Admiration and the Call for No Followers / Darwin and the Rewriting of History (基督教作为奴隶道德与对平等的批判 / 钦佩与对无追随者的呼吁 / 达尔文与历史的改写)

Nietzsche says that all of our moral systems have been made to satisfy lowest common denominator needs. He looks for example at the history of Christianity and says look, the reason why Christianity was a religion of slaves is because it serves the interests of slaves. If you are a Roman master, as Nietzsche kind of likes and sympathizes with, well, you could do all kinds of cruel and wicked stuff, and because you were superior, you never felt bad about it. You didn't have this nasty religious guilt. What kind of a superior heroic man would do that? Nietzsche said that all of Western heroism was poisoned by the advent of Christianity because it made superior people develop what he called...

尼采说，我们所有的道德体系都是为了满足最底层共同需求而制定的。他以基督教的历史为例，说：看，基督教之所以是奴隶的宗教，是因为它服务于奴隶的利益。如果你是罗马的主人，尼采对此颇为欣赏和同情，那么你可以做各种残忍邪恶的事情，而因为你优越，你从不为此感到内疚。你没有那种令人讨厌的宗教负罪感。什么样的优越英雄式人物会那样做呢？尼采说，所有西方的英雄主义都被基督教的到来毒化了，因为它使优越的人发展出他所谓的……

A conscience. And he thought that was a very bad development because that truncated their ability to act. Who knows what great figures would have done if they had not been kept back and held back by these arbitrary strictures of Christian morality. He once wrote a very telling line. He said, I will never forgive Christianity for what it did to Pascal. Now, if you know Blaise Pascal and his agony over religious questions, he says, look, here we have a superior man. He was a great mathematician and a great scientist. What did you do? You set his own mind against his rational abilities, and you destroyed that man. I would give a million Christians who think that they're going to be saved for one more Blaise Pascal, and then I'd be able to tell him that you need to be liberated from these Christian myths, and when you are, then you will truly be the heroic man you could become. So he has an axe to grind against the entire tradition of Western Christianity. He thinks that it inhibits the growth of individuality and it prevents the growth of heroism. And heroism is what he's all about.

……良知。他认为这是一种非常糟糕的发展，因为它限制了他们行动的能力。谁知道那些伟大的人物如果没有被基督教道德这些任意的约束所阻碍和束缚，会做出什么样的事情来。他曾写过一句非常耐人寻味的话。他说：“我永远不会原谅基督教对帕斯卡所做的一切。”如果你们了解布莱兹·帕斯卡及其在宗教问题上的痛苦，他说：看，这里有一个优越的人。他是一位伟大的数学家和科学家。你们做了什么？你们让他自己的心智与他的理性能力作对，你们毁掉了那个人。我愿意用一百万个自以为会得救的基督徒，来换取另一个布莱兹·帕斯卡，然后我就能告诉他，你需要从这些基督教神话中解放出来，当你解放出来时，你才能真正成为你本可以成为的英雄人物。因此，他对整个西方基督教传统怀有怨恨。他认为基督教抑制了个性的发展，阻碍了英雄主义的成长。而英雄主义正是他所追求的核心。

He says, look, I don't care about the mass of human beings. He thinks that the mass of human beings, in opposition to Christianity, he thinks that the mass of human beings have no value whatever. The only people who are worth anything are superior people, and I have a strong suspicion that Nietzsche thought he was one of those superior people. Something you should consider in the history of philosophy, whenever someone makes an argument for aristocracy, they always think that they're aristocrats. You never see plebeians making arguments in favor of philosophical aristocracy. If you see an argument in favor of elitism, the speaker thinks he is part of that elite. Without fail, it always works.

他说：看，我不在乎人类的大多数。与基督教相反，他认为人类的大多数毫无价值。唯一有价值的人是优越的人，而我强烈怀疑尼采认为自己就是那些优越者之一。在哲学史中，你应该考虑一点：每当有人为贵族制辩护时，他们总认为自己就是贵族。你永远不会看到平民为哲学贵族制辩护。如果你看到为精英主义辩护的论证，那么说话者认为自己属于那个精英群体。无一例外，总是如此。

Now Nietzsche, strangely enough, would not feel bad about that. He said, yes, of course I'm a member of an elite. I don't buy this Christian equality of all souls. Look, there's no God for us all to be equal in front of, and big fish eat little fish. Some people are better and more capable than others. We just live in the domain of nature. Stop trying to even things out and help the feeble and the incapable and the incapacitated. Help the great people do great things and to hell with the rest of humanity. It's a radical... courageous but also slightly, slightly immoral, actually even slightly more, greatly immoral stance.

奇怪的是，尼采对此并不会感到愧疚。他说：是的，我当然属于精英。我不相信基督教那种灵魂平等的说法。看，没有上帝让我们在他面前平等，而且大鱼吃小鱼。有些人比其他人更好、更有能力。我们只是生活在自然领域。不要试图拉平一切，帮助那些软弱、无能和无能为力的人。帮助伟大的人做伟大的事，让其余的人类见鬼去吧。这是一种激进的……勇敢但同时也有些许不道德，实际上甚至更多，非常不道德的立场。

In some ways what's heroic about Nietzsche is that his immorality is done at the level of Milton's Satan. He will not serve. And there's something strangely attractive about him for that reason. There are times when he comes up with all sorts of repulsive stuff. But I cannot eliminate from my mind a certain sort of admiration for Nietzsche. I would not be a Nietzschean follower. Nietzsche is the sort of philosopher that if you follow him, you are not at all a Nietzschean.

在某种程度上，尼采的英雄之处在于，他的不道德是在弥尔顿的撒旦层面上进行的。他不愿侍奉。因此，他有一种奇怪的吸引力。有时他会提出各种令人反感的东西。但我无法从脑海中消除对尼采的某种钦佩。我不会成为尼采的追随者。尼采是那种哲学家，如果你追随他，你就根本不是尼采主义者。

Nietzsche wants no followers. As he says, be a man and follow me not. I found my path, you find yours. Anyone who reads all of Nietzsche's books and says, I believe everything Nietzsche told me, understood nothing that he read. The whole point is that he says, go think these problems through for yourself. Like Kierkegaard, he forces you to become an individual. I think it is a good and necessary tonic, but I would worry if anyone came to me and said, after reading this.

尼采不希望有追随者。正如他所说，做一个男子汉，不要跟随我。我找到了我的路，你去找你的路。任何人读完了尼采所有的书，然后说：我相信尼采告诉我的一切，那他就什么也没读懂。关键在于，他说，自己去思考这些问题。像克尔凯郭尔一样，他迫使你成为一个个体。我认为这是一剂良好且必要的补药，但如果有人读完这些后来对我说这话，我会担心。

It seems to me that almost all this is correct. Well, maybe it is, maybe it isn't. But the point is, it is stimulating and provocative. We'll give you interesting questions to ask, if not satisfactory answers to those questions. Now, in a piece called The Use and Abuse of History, he gave his conception of what human life was like, and he gave his conception of the value of human life, and amazingly enough, it's not very high.

在我看来，几乎所有这些说法都是正确的。嗯，也许是对的，也许不对。但关键在于，它刺激且引人深思。我们会给你提出有趣的问题，即使不能给你满意的答案。现在，在一篇名为《历史的用途与滥用》的文章中，他阐述了他对人类生活的看法，以及他对人类生活价值的看法，令人惊讶的是，这个价值并不高。

Remember, he is living in the age of Darwin. Darwin has published The Origin of Species, and there's been a great to-do about it. And some people were trying to find some way of splicing Darwin together with the tradition of Western Christianity. And others were trying to say, well, look, we can accept Darwin, but there's no real big change. We can hold on to the essence of Christianity and just give up those silly myths. Nietzsche says, nay, you can do nothing of the kind. Darwin has fundamentally changed our understanding of ourselves.

记住，他生活在达尔文的时代。达尔文已经出版了《物种起源》，引起了轩然大波。有些人试图找到某种方式将达尔文与西方基督教传统结合起来。另一些人则试图说，看，我们可以接受达尔文，但不会有真正的重大改变。我们可以坚持基督教的本质，只放弃那些愚蠢的神话。尼采说，不，你们绝不能这样做。达尔文已经从根本上改变了我们对自己的理解。

we can never go back. Darwin has conclusively proven that human beings are a part of nature. They are not part of God's providential plan. There was no such thing as Adam and Eve in the Garden of Eden. In fact, all we are are complicated apes.

我们永远无法回头。达尔文已经确凿地证明，人类是自然的一部分。他们不属于上帝的天意计划。伊甸园中的亚当和夏娃根本不存在。事实上，我们不过是复杂的猿类。

He looks at the whole history of human beings and at the status of history and says, we're going to have to rewrite our history. And in the future, the universal historian will not start, say, in ancient Babylon or ancient Egypt, certainly won't start in the Garden of Eden. In the future, the universal historian will start with something on the order of pond scum.

他审视了人类的全部历史和历史的地位，说我们将不得不重写我们的历史。在未来，普遍历史学家不会从古代巴比伦或古埃及开始，当然也不会从伊甸园开始。在未来，普遍历史学家将从某种类似池塘浮渣的东西开始。

### 6. The Will to Power as Fundamental Motive / Christianity as Origin of Modern Science and Its Irony / End of Sonderstellung and Death of God (权力意志作为根本动机 / 基督教作为现代科学的起源及其反讽 / 特殊地位的终结与上帝之死)

That green one-celled stuff that grows on stagnant water, said, in fact, Darwin has shown that that's what our ancestors are. Like it or not, we're a really complicated version of that green stuff that grows on ponds. Now the difference between us and that green stuff is that we've somehow gotten the bizarre idea that the entire universe revolves around us. He says, I have an idea. No, it doesn't. You're a really complicated and really arrogant pond scum that has an entire misapprehension of the nature of the world around you. And once Darwin comes, we can never go back. God is dead. It's just that none of you people have figured that out. And the way in which he introduces that is quite remarkable. He says, look.

那种生长在死水上的绿色单细胞物质，他说，事实上，达尔文已经表明那是我们的祖先。不管你喜不喜欢，我们是那种生长在池塘里的绿色物质的极其复杂的版本。现在，我们与那种绿色物质的区别在于，我们不知何故产生了一种奇怪的想法，认为整个宇宙都围绕我们转。他说，我有一个想法。不，不是这样。你是一种极其复杂且极其傲慢的池塘浮渣，对周围世界的本质有着完全的误解。一旦达尔文出现，我们就永远无法回头。上帝已死。只是你们这些人还没有意识到这一点。而他引入这一观点的方式非常引人注目。他说，看。

Nietzsche thinks that he's found a natural motive within all living things. In other words, he thinks it's an exercise in biology.
