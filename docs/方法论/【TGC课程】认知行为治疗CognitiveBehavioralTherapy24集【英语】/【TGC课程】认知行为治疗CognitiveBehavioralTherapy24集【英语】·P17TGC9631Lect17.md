### 7. Mindfulness-Based Relapse Prevention (MBRP) (正念-based 复发预防（MBRP）)

The next type of therapy that I wanted to talk about is similar to some of the things that we've talked about in the past when we've talked about cognitive therapy in contrast to mindfulness-based cognitive therapy for the treatment of depression. But here we're talking about substance abuse. Cognitive behavioral therapy is still a leading player, but we've added something now called mindfulness-based relapse prevention or MBRP.

接下来我想讨论的另一种疗法，类似于我们过去在讨论抑郁症治疗时提到的认知疗法与基于正念的认知疗法的对比。但这里我们讨论的是物质滥用。认知行为疗法仍然是一个主要的参与者，但我们现在增加了一种叫做基于正念的复发预防（MBRP）的方法。

This was initially developed by Alan Marlott in Washington State and is currently being studied by Sarah Bowen. And here I really want you to think back to those third wave therapies with their focus on mindfulness and sometimes accepting the storms that come in, the cravings that might come in in the terms of substance abuse and not necessarily wrestling with them but just letting them diminish and to leave on their own. So in MBRP, they use the acronym SOBER, S-O-B-E-R, where the S stands for stop, the O stands for observe, the B stands for breathe, the E stands for expand awareness, and the R stands for respond mindfully.

这种方法最初由华盛顿州的艾伦·马拉特（Alan Marlatt）开发，目前由莎拉·鲍文（Sarah Bowen）进行研究。在这里，我真的希望你们回想那些第三代疗法，它们关注正念，有时接受来临的风暴，在物质滥用方面可能出现的渴求，不一定与它们搏斗，而是让它们自行减弱和消失。所以在MBRP中，他们使用缩写SOBER，S-O-B-E-R，其中S代表停止（Stop），O代表观察（Observe），B代表呼吸（Breathe），E代表扩展意识（Expand awareness），R代表正念回应（Respond mindfully）。

So if we think about substance use disorders, an individual will oftentimes see a trigger. That trigger may activate a physiologic or a psychologic craving to reach out and smoke that cigarette, to have that drink, to use drugs. What sober does is it teaches an individual to slow down. It gives them that couple second pause. So that their frontal lobes, that executive functioning can kick in and say, wait a minute, is this really what you want to do? Is this in service of your values and your goals? Or can we have you step outside of the situation or maybe call somebody for help?

因此，如果我们考虑物质使用障碍，一个人通常会看到一个触发因素。那个触发因素可能激活生理或心理上的渴求，促使他们伸手去抽那支烟，喝那杯酒，或使用药物。SOBER所做的是教导一个人放慢速度。它给了他们那几秒钟的停顿。这样他们的前额叶，即执行功能，可以介入并说：等一下，这真的是你想做的吗？这是否符合你的价值观和目标？或者我们能否让你脱离这个情境，或者打电话给别人寻求帮助？

Now, an example of a strategy often used as part of mindfulness-based relapse prevention is something called surfing the urge, and it's something that we use with Michael. And just to remind you, Michael used to be a pack-a-day smoker, so he was physiologically addicted to nicotine. When he stopped smoking, he would have cravings for nicotine.

现在，作为基于正念的复发预防的一部分，经常使用的一个策略例子叫做“冲浪渴望”（surfing the urge），我们用它来帮助迈克尔。提醒你一下，迈克尔曾经每天抽一包烟，所以他在生理上对尼古丁上瘾。当他戒烟时，他会有对尼古丁的渴求。

Now, oftentimes when an individual has a physiologic craving, they imagine that the intensity of that craving will just continue to go up and up and up and up until they just can't stand it anymore. But we know that the way cravings work, yes, there is a physiologic increase to that craving, but it hits a plateau. It stays at that plateau for a relatively short period of time, then that craving begins to decrease. So really all you need to do is to buckle your seatbelt while you're having that craving, and this too shall pass. So surfing the urge first teaches the skills of mindfulness and of awareness, but it tells the individual you have this wave coming in.

现在，通常当一个人有生理渴求时，他们想象那种渴求的强度会一直上升，直到他们无法忍受。但我们知道渴求的工作方式，是的，渴求有生理上的增加，但它会达到一个平台期。它在那个平台期保持相对较短的时间，然后渴求开始减少。所以实际上你只需要在经历渴求时系好安全带，这也会过去。所以“冲浪渴望”首先教导正念和觉察的技能，但它告诉个体：你有一个波浪正在到来。

那么，关于基于正念的复发预防的证据如何？这是莎拉·鲍文在2014年发表的一项研究。她观察了268名物质使用障碍患者，并研究了三种不同的治疗方法。她研究了基于认知或认知行为的、侧重于复发预防的疗法。她研究了基于正念的复发预防，以及常规治疗。

For individuals in aftercare following initial treatment for substance use disorders, CBT relapse prevention and mindfulness-based relapse prevention were superior to treatment as usual. At 12-month follow-up, mindfulness-based relapse prevention offered an added benefit over CBT and treatment as usual in reducing drug use and heavy drinking.

对于在物质使用障碍初步治疗后接受后续护理的个体，CBT复发预防和基于正念的复发预防优于常规治疗。在12个月的随访中，基于正念的复发预防在减少药物使用和重度饮酒方面比CBT和常规治疗提供了额外的益处。

### 8. Therapeutic Education System (TES) and Contingency Management (治疗教育系统（TES）和应急管理)

So it looks like maybe targeted mindfulness practices can support long-term outcomes by strengthening an individual's ability to monitor and skillfully cope with discomfort that comes from those cravings. The next system that I wanted to introduce is something called TES, T-E-S, which stands for the Therapeutic Education System. And I wanted to illustrate this because it's delivered in a different modality.

所以看起来，有针对性的正念练习可以通过增强个体监测和巧妙应对来自渴求的不适感的能力，来支持长期结果。接下来我想介绍的系统叫做TES，T-E-S，代表治疗教育系统（Therapeutic Education System）。我想说明这一点，因为它以不同的方式提供。

In tests, we have 62 internet modules, so it's quite extensive. Again, as a CBT person, I see them and I think, this is lots of CBT, but it's based primarily on CRA, the Community Reinforcement Approach, developed by Lisa Marsh and her colleagues at Dartmouth University, and recently evaluated in a paper by Campbell in 2014, where they looked at 507 individuals using alcohol and drugs and working their way through these 62 internet modules. What they found was that TESS was superior to treatment as usual on both adherence and abstinence. So it looks like their treatment approach, CRA, delivered through an internet modality was helpful and successful.

在TES中，我们有62个互联网模块，所以相当广泛。同样，作为CBT从业者，我看到它们，我想，这有很多CBT，但它主要基于CRA，即社区强化方法（Community Reinforcement Approach），由丽莎·马什（Lisa Marsh）和她在达特茅斯大学的同事开发，最近在坎贝尔（Campbell）2014年的一篇论文中进行了评估，他们观察了507名使用酒精和药物的人，通过这62个互联网模块进行学习。他们发现，TES在依从性和戒断方面都优于常规治疗。所以看起来他们的治疗方法CRA，通过互联网方式提供，是有帮助且成功的。

Now, one of the interesting features of TESS is that it uses a contingency management system to get people to do the modules. So essentially what happens is every time you use the module, you get a sort of lottery ticket that makes you eligible to win, say, $100 in prize money. Each time you take another module, you get another ticket and your odds continue to go up. There's a number of different variants on contingency management where you use lotteries or rewards or payments as a reward for someone who, say, comes in and gives a urine sample and you can prove that they haven't been using. But does contingency management work? And should we pay people to not engage in unhealthy behaviors? I want to share just a couple of studies on this topic. The first is by Farnado in 2013, and they did a systematic review of CBT versus contingency management for cocaine addiction.

现在，TESS的一个有趣特点是它使用应急管理（contingency management）系统来促使人们完成模块。基本上，每次你使用模块，就会得到一张类似彩票的凭证，有机会赢得比如100美元的奖金。每完成一个模块，你就多一张彩票，中奖几率也随之增加。应急管理有多种变体，比如通过彩票、奖励或报酬来激励那些前来提供尿样并证明自己未吸毒的人。但应急管理真的有效吗？我们是否应该付钱让人们避免不健康的行为？我想分享几项关于这个主题的研究。第一项是Farnado在2013年进行的，他们对可卡因成瘾的认知行为疗法（CBT）与应急管理进行了系统综述。

They found that contingency management produces very rapid results in the beginning of treatment, and CBT produced results towards the end and after treatment had ended. About half of the studies that did a combination of the two showed better results, but half of the studies showed that a combination didn't really matter and they weren't very supportive of contingency management.

他们发现，应急管理在治疗初期能迅速产生效果，而CBT的效果则在治疗后期及治疗结束后显现。在将两者结合的研究中，约有一半显示效果更佳，但另一半则显示结合并无显著差异，且对应急管理支持不足。

In another study done looking at smoking, it was a Cochrane review by Cahill and Pereira in 2011, they looked at 19 different studies, a total of about 4,500 different subjects. There was only one large study out of their 19 studies showed that payments for abstinence were beneficial. The incentives did increase participation, but the initial success faded after the rewards and they went away.

另一项关于戒烟的研究是Cahill和Pereira在2011年进行的Cochrane综述，他们分析了19项研究，共约4500名受试者。在这19项研究中，只有一项大型研究表明为戒断提供报酬是有益的。激励措施确实提高了参与度，但初期成功在奖励结束后便逐渐消失。

### 9. CRAFT and Practical Strategies for Cutting Down (CRAFT与减少使用的实用策略)

So it looks like incentives are just one extrinsic motivator, and it looks like the results are mixed for now. However, a more common scenario is when a family or friend is trying to get their loved one to cut down or to quit using entirely. It's estimated that for every one substance abuser, there are five family members affected. So what should families do?

因此，激励似乎只是一种外在动机，目前结果好坏参半。然而，更常见的情况是家人或朋友试图让亲人减少或完全停止使用毒品。据估计，每名物质滥用者会影响五名家庭成员。那么，家人应该怎么做呢？

Now the last modality I wanted to talk about is something called CRAFT. Now this is CRA as we've talked about before, but they've added an FT which stands for family training. Now CRAFT is a more recent outgrowth of CRA that focuses on how to help the loved ones of those abusing alcohol or drugs. Now I found a couple of books and websites about CRAFT that I thought were good and quite helpful. One was by Jeff Foote called Beyond Addiction.

现在我想讨论的最后一种方法是CRAFT。这是我们之前提到的CRA，但增加了FT，代表家庭训练（Family Training）。CRAFT是CRA的一个较新分支，专注于帮助那些滥用酒精或药物者的亲人。我找到了一些关于CRAFT的书籍和网站，觉得不错且很有帮助。一本是Jeff Foote的《超越成瘾》（Beyond Addiction）。

Another by Robert Myers called Get Your Loved One Sober. There's a website called The 20-Minute Guide with 20 using the numbers to zero. This website includes instructions, motivational messages about how to take care of yourself, how to communicate with your loved one, and how to get help if you need it. Now, the goal of CRAFT is to use positive communication to reinforce positive behaviors while taking care of yourself and using other social supports. You want to highlight positive interactions, positive conversations, times when your loved one is sober or not using, times when they've been reliable. Some might say, is this enabling? But remember that there's an important distinction. When you are enabling, you're increasing an individual's drug use behavior by removing negative consequences. You cover for them at work. You pay for them to have their car fixed, which they crashed when they were using. That might be enabling. A positive reinforcement increases non-drinking behavior. And thus far, the research results are positive.

另一本是Robert Myers的《让你的亲人清醒》（Get Your Loved One Sober）。还有一个网站叫“20分钟指南”（The 20-Minute Guide），其中“20”用数字零表示。该网站包含指导、激励信息，教你如何照顾自己、如何与亲人沟通，以及如何在需要时寻求帮助。CRAFT的目标是通过积极沟通来强化积极行为，同时照顾好自己并利用其他社会支持。你要突出积极互动、积极对话，以及亲人清醒或未使用毒品、表现可靠的时刻。有人可能会问，这是否是在纵容？但请记住，两者有重要区别。纵容是通过消除负面后果来增加个体的吸毒行为。比如你为他们的工作打掩护，或为他们支付因吸毒撞坏的车修理费，那可能是纵容。而积极强化则增加非饮酒行为。迄今为止，研究结果是积极的。

Let's move on to ask one of our frequently asked questions. And this patient asks, other than surfing the urge to deal with cravings, what are some other strategies to cut down? Well, it really depends on the substance and it depends on the severity of use. But the first thing that I would do is self-monitoring or some sort of substance use diary where you not only want to record use, but you want to record the antecedents or the triggers as well as the consequences. You might then think about removing triggers. If an individual is triggered because they drive by a bar on their way home from work, change the route that you drive so that you are able to avoid that trigger.

接下来我们回答一个常见问题。这位患者问：除了应对渴求的“冲浪”技巧，还有哪些减少使用的策略？这确实取决于所使用物质和使用严重程度。但我会首先建议自我监测或某种物质使用日记，不仅要记录使用情况，还要记录前因（或触发因素）以及后果。然后可以考虑消除触发因素。如果一个人因为下班开车经过酒吧而被触发，可以改变行车路线以避免该触发因素。

You might also make some temporary or even permanent changes in your social circle. Remember, our friends and family have a big influence on the behavioral choices we make. If they're pushing you in unhealthy directions, maybe it's time to have different social supports. Another helpful website to go to, and this is particularly for folks who might be in that at-risk drinking category, is called the Drinkers Checkup, which you can find on Google.

你可能还需要在社交圈做出暂时甚至永久的改变。记住，朋友和家人对我们行为选择有很大影响。如果他们把你推向不健康的方向，也许是时候寻找不同的社会支持了。另一个有用的网站，特别是针对可能处于风险饮酒类别的人，是“饮酒者检查”（Drinkers Checkup），你可以在谷歌上找到。
