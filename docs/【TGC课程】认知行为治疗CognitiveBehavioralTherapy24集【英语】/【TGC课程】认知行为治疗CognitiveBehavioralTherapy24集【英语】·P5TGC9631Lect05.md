那么应对策略的选择呢？这里我们又在谈论那些行为了。应激源发生，你有你的评估，你有压力反应，然后你做些什么。也许有帮助，也许没有帮助。但我们把应对行为分为两类。一类侧重于改变你的情绪状态，帮助你感觉更好。躯体静默就属于这一类。另一类是问题聚焦的。所以你可以卷起袖子，开始为让你感到压力的考试而学习。这就是情绪聚焦和问题聚焦的应对。两者不一定哪个更好。这取决于当时需要做什么。你需要在两种应对之间取得平衡。

What CBT data does is it looks at your preferred coping styles and see whether or not you're selecting the most adaptive strategies. So you want to select behaviors. You may select behaviors that evoke a relaxation response, emotion-focused coping. You may decide to reach out and ask your social supports for help. That could be emotion-focused coping, but might also be problem-focused coping. You could have task-oriented behaviors. If you're really stressed about the state of your home, you decide to roll up your sleeves and do some home improvements, that would certainly be a problem-focused coping.

CBT数据所做的是查看你偏好的应对风格，看看你是否选择了最具适应性的策略。所以你要选择行为。你可以选择引发放松反应的行为，即情绪聚焦应对。你可以决定向社交支持寻求帮助。这可能是情绪聚焦应对，但也可能是问题聚焦应对。你可以有任务导向的行为。如果你对家里的状况感到压力很大，决定卷起袖子做些家庭改善，那肯定是问题聚焦的应对。

### 9. Research on cognitive behavioral stress management (认知行为压力管理研究)

You could also just decide to schedule pleasant activities, things that bring you a sense of joy, or at least allow you to escape an unchangeable stressor for a short period of time. But does it work? What does the data show us? Now most of the high quality research in the stress management cognitive therapy realm is done by Mike Antoni who's at the University of Miami. Essentially wrote the book on what's called cognitive behavioral stress management or CBSM. He's done a number of studies of regular folks but also individuals that have chronic medical stressors such as HIV, cancer, or chronic fatigue.

你也可以决定安排一些愉快的活动，那些能带给你快乐感，或者至少让你在短时间内逃离无法改变的压力源的事情。但这有效吗？数据告诉了我们什么？目前，在压力管理认知疗法领域，大多数高质量的研究是由迈阿密大学的迈克·安东尼（Mike Antoni）完成的。他基本上撰写了关于所谓认知行为压力管理（CBSM）的权威著作。他对普通人以及患有慢性医学压力源（如HIV、癌症或慢性疲劳）的个体进行了多项研究。

Now the most common outcomes he's looking at are depression, anxiety, quality of life, and for folks with medical conditions, he's sometimes looking at adherence to treatment or the progression or slowing of their particular illness. What he's found is fairly strong support for a mix of cognitive restructuring, wrestling with those thoughts, of relaxation, coping skills, and also assertiveness, and particularly being able to and feeling entitled to ask for the support that you need.

他最常关注的结果是抑郁、焦虑、生活质量，而对于患有医学状况的人，他有时会关注治疗依从性或他们特定疾病的进展或延缓。他发现，相当有力的证据支持将认知重构（即与那些想法搏斗）、放松、应对技巧以及自信（尤其是能够并感到有权去寻求你需要的支持）结合起来。

In a recent interesting study of CBSM, he looked at women with breast cancer with Stegall and other colleagues in 2014. They did a nice long five-year follow-up using CBSM and found that in these women with breast cancer who underwent this particular treatment, they had less depression, they had higher quality of life.

在最近一项关于CBSM的有趣研究中，他与Stegall及其他同事在2014年研究了患有乳腺癌的女性。他们进行了长达五年的随访，使用CBSM，发现这些接受这种特定治疗的乳腺癌女性抑郁程度较低，生活质量较高。

### 10. Mindfulness-based stress reduction and resilience (正念减压与心理韧性)

I wanted to end by talking about a different form of, I guess we would call it a third wave type of stress reduction intervention. It's something you might have heard of. It's called MBSR or mindfulness-based stress reduction. Mindfulness-based stress reduction is a modern variant of meditation and yoga that has been applied to stress reduction. Like meditation, it builds concentration, present focus, acceptance, and somatic quieting, all probably familiar at this point. It was developed by John Cabot Zinn.

最后，我想谈谈一种不同形式的干预，我想我们可以称之为第三波减压干预。你可能听说过它，叫做MBSR或正念减压。正念减压是一种现代变体的冥想和瑜伽，已被应用于减压。像冥想一样，它培养专注力、当下意识、接纳和躯体平静，这些在这一点上可能都很熟悉。它由约翰·卡巴特-津（John Kabat-Zinn）开发。

at the University of Massachusetts and has since been applied to a wide array of different medical problems, but it all started with chronic pain. Now most MBSR programs last about eight to ten weeks. They consist of two and a half hour weekly classes and oftentimes will have a single day or maybe a weekend retreat at the end. It's not particularly spiritually based, but there's certainly a Buddhist element and Buddhist roots to the mindfulness that these individuals are learning.

在马萨诸塞大学，此后已应用于各种不同的医学问题，但一切都始于慢性疼痛。现在大多数MBSR项目持续约八到十周。它们包括每周两个半小时的课程，并且通常在结束时会有单日或周末静修。它并不是特别基于灵性，但这些人所学习的正念肯定有佛教元素和佛教根源。

Now, MBSR has been shown to improve chronic pain, low back pain, coping with pain in general, stress, mood, and even an interesting study looking at improvements in immune function.

现在，MBSR已被证明可以改善慢性疼痛、下背痛、一般疼痛应对、压力、情绪，甚至一项有趣的研究显示可以改善免疫功能。

I wanted to share just one meta-analysis published in 2004 by Paul Grossman and his colleagues in the Journal of Psychosomatic Research. What they did was to look at 20 high-quality MBSR studies that covered a wide spectrum of patients, cancer, pain, cardiovascular disease, depression, anxiety, and they looked at the standardized measures of physical and mental well-being. And across the board, they found moderate to strong effect sizes. So it looks like...

我想分享一项由保罗·格罗斯曼（Paul Grossman）及其同事于2004年发表在《心身研究杂志》上的元分析。他们所做的是考察了20项高质量的MBSR研究，这些研究涵盖了广泛的病人群体，包括癌症、疼痛、心血管疾病、抑郁、焦虑，他们考察了身心健康的标准测量。总体而言，他们发现了中等到强的效应量。所以看起来……

There's really something there to MBSR. And I would encourage you, if you're interested, just to Google online mindfulness-based stress reduction tools. And a number of tools, videos, and workbooks will pop up. So let's wrap up just by talking about one of our frequently asked questions. And this is a question that I often get. Is resilience to stress an enduring characteristic? Or is it something that comes and goes? How can I make it stay?

MBSR确实有它的价值。如果你感兴趣，我鼓励你上网搜索“正念减压工具”。会出现许多工具、视频和工作手册。那么让我们通过讨论一个常见问题来总结。这是我经常被问到的问题：对压力的韧性是一种持久的特质吗？还是它会来去匆匆？我怎样才能让它保持？

Well, I think it's a little bit both. I think it's partly dispositional, and maybe that has to do with our genetics. Maybe it has to do with early life experiences. But I do think that resilience and certainly cognitive-based stress management are skills that we can learn. We can, in essence, build up our strength. We can build up our stamina. We can build up our endurance, especially if we know that very stressful times are to come. Now, one other important point that I want to make is just because an individual is sensitive, it doesn't mean that they're fragile.

嗯，我认为两者兼有。我认为部分是天生的，也许这与我们的基因有关。也许这与早期生活经历有关。但我确实认为韧性，当然还有基于认知的压力管理，是我们能学习的技能。我们本质上可以增强我们的力量。我们可以增强我们的耐力。我们可以增强我们的持久力，尤其是当我们知道压力很大的时期即将到来时。现在，我想提出的另一个重要观点是，仅仅因为一个人敏感，并不意味着他们脆弱。

For some reason, some individuals, again it might be dispositional, it might be life experiences, they simply feel stress or in general emotions much more intensely than others. That means they have a higher burden in terms of learning emotion regulation skills, but it doesn't mean they're going to break. In fact, some of those sensitive folks might be the strongest people that you'll ever meet. So in summary, we know that stress is normal.

出于某种原因，有些人——同样，可能是天生的，也可能是生活经历——他们只是比其他人更强烈地感受到压力或一般情绪。这意味着他们在学习情绪调节技能方面负担更重，但这并不意味着他们会崩溃。事实上，一些敏感的人可能是你见过的最坚强的人。所以总结一下，我们知道压力是正常的。

But we worry if it's chronic, we worry if it's severe, and we worry if a person has selected maladaptive coping strategies. We want to manage it by looking at appraisals, we want to use somatic quieting, and we certainly want to help people reach out to their social supports.
