### 6. Stress-Induced Analgesia and Chronic Stress / Pharmacotherapy and Chronic Care Model (应激诱导镇痛与慢性应激 / 药物治疗与慢性病护理模式)

Now, relaxation and guided imagery are just one set of tools to address chronic pain. They may work through somatic quieting, but they may also work through their effect in decreasing stress. So let's talk a bit about stress and pain. Now, in conditions of extreme stress like trauma, our experience of pain may be entirely shut down. But even more minor acute stressors are known to produce something called stress-induced analgesia. Our fight-or-flight system and our HPA axis stress response not only lead to the release of epinephrine and cortisol, the stress hormones we've talked about before, they also promote the release of beta-endorphins, our endogenous opiates, or our internal painkillers.

现在，放松和引导想象只是应对慢性疼痛的一组工具。它们可能通过躯体静默起作用，但也可能通过减少压力的效果起作用。所以让我们谈谈压力和疼痛。在极端压力如创伤的情况下，我们的疼痛体验可能完全关闭。但即使是更轻微的急性应激源也被认为会产生所谓的应激诱导镇痛。我们的战斗或逃跑系统和下丘脑-垂体-肾上腺轴应激反应不仅导致肾上腺素和皮质醇的释放，即我们之前讨论过的应激激素，它们还促进β-内啡肽的释放，这是我们内源性的阿片类物质，或者说我们内部的止痛剂。

How do we know this? Well, from early animal research where animals were subjected to various stressors, then blood endorphin levels were measured. In human studies, researchers use things like foot shocks, exercise stress, a social stress test, and other methods to elicit a stress response, measure endorphins, and in some cases block endorphins using a drug called naloxone. It's generally thought to be evolutionary and it's thought to be adaptive. As we've learned, though, our stress response systems and the chemical messengers they release were really intended for short-term situations and not the more common stressors we encounter today, which are often of a chronic nature. Chronic stress does not produce analgesia. In fact, it sometimes produces hyperalgesia or increased sensitivity to pain.

我们如何知道这一点？这源于早期的动物研究，实验中动物被置于各种应激源下，然后测量其血液中的内啡肽水平。在人体研究中，研究人员使用足部电击、运动应激、社会应激测试等方法诱发应激反应，测量内啡肽，有时还会使用一种名为纳洛酮的药物来阻断内啡肽。人们普遍认为这是进化上的结果，并且具有适应性。然而，正如我们所知，我们的应激反应系统及其释放的化学信使原本是为短期情境而设计的，而非我们今天面临的更为常见的应激源，这些应激源往往是慢性的。慢性应激不会产生镇痛效应。事实上，它有时会产生痛觉过敏，即对疼痛的敏感性增加。

We don't fully understand how this happens and there are most likely a number of different mechanisms, most of which include some sort of structural rewiring of the central nervous system triggered by the higher levels of glucocorticoids or those stress hormones which we've mentioned before. Now, if stress seems to be playing a major role, then it's time to move to our strategies for stress management. This might include various somatic quieting, working with primary or secondary appraisals, the cognitions about the stressor or your coping resources. We might encourage a person to have better use of those coping resources or social supports, or adding in new activities that help to mitigate the effects of stress. So, how do we treat pain?

我们尚未完全理解其发生机制，很可能涉及多种不同的机制，其中大多数包括由较高水平的糖皮质激素或我们之前提到的应激激素触发的中枢神经系统某种结构性重塑。现在，如果应激似乎起着主要作用，那么是时候转向我们的压力管理策略了。这可能包括各种躯体放松技巧、处理初级或次级评估、关于应激源或应对资源的认知。我们可能会鼓励个体更好地利用应对资源或社会支持，或者增加有助于减轻压力影响的新活动。那么，我们如何治疗疼痛呢？

Well, most people immediately think of pharmacotherapy. And these are the drugs in the opioid family, usually like morphine or hydrocodone or oxycodone. And these drugs work because of their similarity to our body's own pain relievers, the endogenous opioids that we've talked about before. But let's expand our idea of treatment and let's look at several non-pharmacologic treatments.

大多数人会立刻想到药物治疗。这些是阿片类药物，通常如吗啡、氢可酮或羟考酮。这些药物之所以有效，是因为它们与我们身体自身的止痛剂——我们之前讨论过的内源性阿片类物质——具有相似性。但让我们扩展治疗思路，看看几种非药物治疗。

Now, recall the need for a new attitude towards the management of chronic disease when we talked about the chronic care model. We wanted to encourage more patient empowerment and patient self-management. And if we think of chronic pain as a chronic disease, it would certainly fit into that category that might benefit from the chronic care model.

现在，回想我们在讨论慢性病照护模式时提到的对慢性病管理新态度的需求。我们希望鼓励更多的患者赋权和患者自我管理。如果我们把慢性疼痛视为一种慢性疾病，它当然属于可能受益于慢性病照护模式的类别。

Remember one of the core skills was in self-monitoring, but in this case we would be creating a self-monitoring form like a pain diary. We would need to create that diary to collect data and then to create a treatment package.

记住核心技能之一是自我监测，但在这种情况下，我们会创建一个自我监测表格，比如疼痛日记。我们需要创建该日记来收集数据，然后制定一个治疗方案。

So what would be in a treatment package? What sorts of things would we want to piece together? Well, we might have pharmacotherapy as one tool in our toolkit to combat chronic pain, but we might also want to think about practical, structural, or even mechanical interventions.

那么治疗方案中会包含什么呢？我们想要拼凑哪些内容？我们可能会将药物治疗作为对抗慢性疼痛的工具箱中的一种工具，但我们也可能要考虑实际的、结构性的甚至机械性的干预措施。

So that would be things like physical therapy, occupational therapy, changes in your environment, having pole bars in your home, using walkers, using braces or wraps, using ights, using heat, using massage. All of those things packaged together can have a fairly substantial impact depending on the individual's pain and the source of pain.

这些包括物理治疗、职业治疗、环境改变、在家中安装扶手杆、使用助行器、使用支具或护具、使用冰敷、热敷、按摩。所有这些组合在一起，根据个体的疼痛及其来源，可能会产生相当显著的效果。

### 7. Cognitive Work: Expectations, Attitudes, and ABCD (认知工作：期望、态度与ABCD)

But part of that treatment package should also include the psychological. And in that package we would want to include some of the cognitive strategies that we know about. Well first we want to look at an individual's expectations. Expectations for their level of functioning, expectations for their pain, but also expectations for their pain control strategies. When a patient tells me that their goal is to feel numb all of the time, I get very worried because first of all it's an unrealistic goal and secondly I wonder if maybe they're looking for that numbness as a way to avoid something that might be more psychologically painful.

但治疗方案的一部分还应包括心理方面。在该方案中，我们希望纳入我们所知的一些认知策略。首先，我们要关注个体的期望。对其功能水平的期望、对疼痛的期望，还有对其疼痛控制策略的期望。当患者告诉我他们的目标是始终感觉麻木时，我非常担心，因为首先这是一个不切实际的目标，其次我怀疑他们是否在寻求那种麻木感以逃避可能更具心理痛苦的事情。

We would also want to look at an individual's attitude. Had they become helpless or hopeless or even depressed as a consequence of the pain that they've been experiencing, maybe over a long period of time? We might want to look at and change some or challenge some maladaptive cognitions. Recall those habits of mind that we've talked about before. Is an individual catastrophizing? Are they using all-or-none thinking? Are they using maximizing or minimizing? Or another habit of mind that we haven't talked about much is called emotional reasoning, where an individual deduces, again on an implicit level, that if I feel something with a really strong emotional charge, that must mean it's true.

我们还要关注个体的态度。他们是否因为长期经历的疼痛而变得无助、绝望甚至抑郁？我们可能想要审视并改变或挑战一些适应不良的认知。回想我们之前讨论过的思维习惯。个体是否在灾难化？是否使用全有或全无思维？是否使用夸大或缩小？或者另一种我们较少讨论的思维习惯称为情绪化推理，即个体在隐含层面上推断，如果我对某事有强烈的情感反应，那必定意味着它是真的。

Actually, it just means you have a strong emotional charge and it's going to be a little bit harder to step outside of that emotion and to rationally look at a particular situation. Then of course we want to use some of those cognitive strategies for stress management where we might look at accuracy of appraisals. So let's take a moment then to look at what a cognitive exercise might look like if we are looking at the habits of mind of an individual who has chronic pain.

实际上，这仅仅意味着你有强烈的情感反应，要跳出这种情绪并理性地看待特定情境会有点困难。然后，当然，我们想要使用一些认知策略进行压力管理，比如检查评估的准确性。那么，让我们花点时间看看，如果我们审视一个慢性疼痛患者的思维习惯，认知练习会是什么样子。

Let's say that we're doing an ABCD where we look at the activating event, the beliefs, the consequences again which are both emotions and behavior, as well as the disputation or this is where you restructure or rework those thoughts. Let's say that the activating event is having a stabbing back pain. It doesn't have to be an external event. It's just whatever triggers this cascade of cognitions and emotions and reactions. So let's say the individual has an activating event of stabbing back pain. It triggers the cognitions or beliefs of, I can't take this. This is horrible. My life has been ruined by this pain.

假设我们进行ABCD分析，其中A是激活事件，B是信念，C是后果（包括情绪和行为），D是辩驳，即你重构或调整这些想法的地方。假设激活事件是背部刺痛。它不一定是外部事件，只是触发一连串认知、情绪和反应的任何事物。假设个体的激活事件是背部刺痛。它触发了这样的认知或信念：“我受不了了。这太可怕了。我的生活被这种疼痛毁了。”

Consequences they have, driven by those beliefs and cognitions, they feel depressed, they feel hopeless, they stay at home, and they decide to take more of their pain pills. If they decide that those consequences are not what they were after, they're not helpful, instead they're hurtful, then you want to go to the D box, the dispute box. And to create that dispute box, remember to think about those habits of mind. Is this individual relying on some of those habits of mind? Are these thoughts balanced? Can you look at the other side of the coin? If you had a friend or a loved one that was saying these things, what might you say in return to help them think about a more balanced approach?

这些信念和认知所导致的后果是，他们感到抑郁、绝望，待在家里，并决定服用更多止痛药。如果他们认定这些后果并非所愿，没有帮助反而有害，那么就要转向D框，即质疑框。要构建这个质疑框，记得要思考那些思维习惯。这个人是否依赖某些思维习惯？这些想法是否平衡？你能看到事物的另一面吗？如果你有朋友或亲人说这些话，你会如何回应，以帮助他们思考一种更平衡的方式？

What would be some more helpful but still accurate and believable cognitions? So the disputations might include things like, yes, this physical pain is intense, but it's familiar, and I have a plan to manage it. I can take my meds, I can stretch, I can use ice, I can use distraction, I can pray, or I can meditate.

哪些会是更有帮助但仍然准确且可信的认知？因此，质疑可能包括：是的，这种身体疼痛很剧烈，但它很熟悉，而且我有应对计划。我可以服药、拉伸、冰敷、分散注意力、祈祷或冥想。

Another dispute might be, I had pain yesterday and I had pain the day before that and the day before that. And you know what? I survived. This stinks, but it's beyond my control. I can manage this. 另一个质疑可能是：我昨天疼，前天也疼，大前天也疼。你知道吗？我挺过来了。这很糟糕，但这不是我能控制的。我能应付。或者另一个例子是：即使我疼，如果我出门去某个地方、完成某件事或见到某人，我总是会感觉好些。孤立也会带来痛苦。我可以选择不孤立自己。

### 8. Behavioral Strategies (行为策略)

If we're still thinking about that pain package, that pain management package, we have our structural, functional, mechanical tools. We have our pharmacotherapy. We have our cognitive therapy. Now we want to add in some additional behavioral strategies. We've mentioned things like physical therapy and massage, which of course can be helpful. We might want to think about something called graded or sometimes called graduated physical activity. And essentially this is exercise starting at a very mild, modest level and slowly working the way up into a person has built more flexibility, strength, endurance. This is something you might start with a physical therapist but then you would continue it on your own.

如果我们仍在考虑那个疼痛管理方案，我们已经有结构性的、功能性的、机械性的工具，有药物治疗，有认知疗法。现在我们要加入一些额外的行为策略。我们提到过物理治疗和按摩，这些当然可能有帮助。我们可能要考虑一种称为分级或渐进式体力活动的方案。本质上，这是从非常温和、适度的运动开始，逐步增加，直到个人建立起更大的灵活性、力量和耐力。这可能需要在物理治疗师指导下开始，但之后你可以自己继续进行。

Another common behavioral activity would be somatic quieting. Remember we think of chronic pain as a chronic stress response. That means that the individual's HPA axis, their sympathetic nervous system, are working on overdrive. So it's going to take an equal amount of counteraction in order to turn down the volume on those overactive stress response systems.

另一种常见的行为活动是躯体静默。记住，我们将慢性疼痛视为一种慢性应激反应。这意味着个体的下丘脑-垂体-肾上腺轴和交感神经系统处于过度运转状态。因此，需要同等程度的对抗措施来调低那些过度活跃的应激反应系统的音量。

Now a critical behavioral element is activity scheduling and if you recall activity scheduling from our depression lectures, we talked about the activity record where an individual writes down their activities, but then they can prospectively plan activities that they know are going to have a lifting effect on their mood. For individuals who are in pain, they may not be depressed, but oftentimes what happens is they find themselves pulling away from sources of enjoyment or support or social relationships.

现在一个关键的行为要素是活动安排。如果你还记得我们在抑郁症讲座中讲过的活动安排，我们谈到了活动记录，即个人写下他们的活动，但他们也可以前瞻性地计划那些他们知道会对情绪有提升作用的活动。对于处于疼痛中的个体，他们可能没有抑郁，但经常发生的情况是，他们发现自己远离了快乐、支持或社交关系的来源。

### 9. Pleasure Predicting and Treatment Planning (愉悦预测与治疗计划)

Remember that lower activity levels mean less reward, less reinforcement, and eventually those might even lead to depression. So we want an individual to start scheduling those positive activities. But what happens and what often comes up when an individual is in pain? And I've seen this many, many times with patients. We'll talk about the relationship between activities and mood. We'll talk about the importance of scheduling activities. But the response I often get is, yes, but I hurt. Yes, I would love to do that, but I have pain. Yes, I could schedule that, but I won't enjoy it because the whole time I'm going to be thinking about my pain.

记住，较低的活动水平意味着较少的奖赏、较少的强化，最终甚至可能导致抑郁。所以我们希望个体开始安排那些积极的活动。但是，当一个人处于疼痛中时，会发生什么，又经常出现什么情况？我在病人身上见过很多很多次。我们会谈论活动与情绪之间的关系，谈论安排活动的重要性。但我经常得到的回应是：是的，但我疼。是的，我很想做那件事，但我有疼痛。是的，我可以安排，但我不会享受，因为整个过程我都会想着我的疼痛。

If you remember with depression, we had a similar objection saying, yeah, I could do that, but I won't enjoy it. So we did something called pleasure predicting. If we can get the patient to suspend their disbelief temporarily, to go ahead and prospectively schedule one thing, I will ask them on a scale of 1 to 10, how much do you predict you're going to enjoy yourself? They make their prediction. They write it down. Then the next week they go and actually do that activity. Once they've finished, they re-rate the level of pleasure that they actually experienced. And almost always, not every time, but almost always they find that they enjoyed it more than they had predicted. Chronic pain, like depression, often causes people to make very pessimistic predictions, and it's a matter of giving them that extra boost they need to go out and test out those assumptions.

如果你记得在抑郁症中，我们有类似的反对意见，说：是的，我可以做，但我不会享受。所以我们做了所谓的愉悦预测。如果我们能让病人暂时搁置他们的怀疑，去前瞻性地安排一件事，我会问他们，在1到10的评分中，你预测自己会有多享受？他们做出预测并写下来。然后下周他们实际去做那项活动。完成后，他们重新评价实际体验到的愉悦程度。几乎总是，虽然不是每次，但他们发现自己比预测的更享受。慢性疼痛和抑郁症一样，常常导致人们做出非常悲观的预测，而给他们额外的推动力去检验这些假设是关键。

### 10. Evidence for CBT and Addressing Doubt / Preview: Relationships and Health (认知行为疗法的证据与质疑应对 / 预告：人际关系与健康)

What does the data show us? If we look at a Cochrane review by Williams, Eccleston, and Morley in 2012, they looked at CBT versus treatment as usual or weightless controls and they found that CBT had statistically significant but small effects on pain and disability, moderate effects on mood and catastrophizing, and by 6 to 12 months the main effect was on mood. So again it's not reaching in and fixing the pain but it's altering and changing an individual's mood and potentially changing some of those habits of mind.

数据告诉了我们什么？如果我们看看威廉姆斯、埃克尔斯顿和莫利在2012年发表的科克伦综述，他们比较了认知行为疗法与常规治疗或等待名单对照组，发现认知行为疗法对疼痛和残疾有统计学上显著但较小的效果，对情绪和灾难化思维有中等效果，而在6至12个月后，主要效果体现在情绪上。所以，这再次说明，它并不是直接修复疼痛，而是改变和调整个体的情绪，并可能改变某些思维习惯。

A Cochrane review of behavioral treatments including CBT for chronic low back pain included 30 randomized controlled trials and concluded that behavioral treatments were more effective than usual care for pain post-treatment but no different in immediate to long-term effects on pain or functional status. A review of behavioral treatments for headaches by Eindrezic in 2007 described CBT-based interventions, relaxation, biofeedback, cognitive therapy, and it was able to reduce headache activity by as much as 30 to 60 percent on average across those different studies. These effects surpassed those of various control conditions and were typically sustained over time, including years after treatment.
