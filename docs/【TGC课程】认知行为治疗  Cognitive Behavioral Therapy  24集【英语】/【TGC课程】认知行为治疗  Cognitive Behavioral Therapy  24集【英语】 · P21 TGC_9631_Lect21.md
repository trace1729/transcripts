- Couple CBT extends individual therapy to include the partner when relational issues are central, focusing on areas like communication, empathy, trust, and forgiveness.
- Couple therapy differs from individual therapy because cognitions are 'hot' and in-session, and SMART goals must account for both partners' potentially conflicting goals.
- Therapy often starts with positive aspects of the relationship, such as recalling what initially attracted partners to each other, before addressing conflict.
- Conflict resolution tools like DESC (Describe, Emote, Specify, Consequences) or EASE (Event, Emotion, Specify) help partners express themselves without escalating blame.
- Assessment uses triangulation of self-report surveys, individual interviews, and joint interviews, complemented by in-session observation of communication.
- Cognitive-behavioral conflict resolution skills have empirical support from studies such as Dunn and Schwebel (1995) and Wood (2005).
